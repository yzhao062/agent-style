## [0.8.0] — 2026-10-17

Minor: the Escape Hatch and the RULE-03, RULE-04, RULE-12, RULE-B, RULE-D, and RULE-G directives change, which `RELEASING.md` counts as a change to the ruleset contract, and the mechanical detectors gain two hook-only functions. The rule count, the rule IDs, the CLI interface, and the published bench scope are unchanged.

### Changed

- **The Escape Hatch says most rules are defaults.** Agent drafts obeyed every default in every sentence, and the result was a new uniformity: almost no connective words, sentences cut to similar lengths, and bold paragraph leads in one pattern. Human writers follow the principles but depart from any single rule now and then, and that variety is part of why their prose reads as written by a person. The section keeps the Orwell quote. It now says that most rules are defaults applied with judgment. Writers depart when the sentence reads better for it, and not to vary the pattern. A few rules are firm (no invented citations, evidence, or numbers; no placeholders in finished text), and a draft is judged by its overall pattern. The compact render says the nine field-observed rules "carry the same weight as" the canonical twelve, where it said they "bind as fully as" them.
- **RULE-D keeps the words that mark how sentences relate, where the text establishes the relation.** The directive still rules out additive-only openers ("Additionally", "Furthermore", "Moreover"). It no longer says connections should usually be implicit. Instead it asks writers to keep contrast, cause, and sequence words ("However", "Thus", "First") that mark a relation the text already establishes, and it carries no rate. A cause word is itself a claim. A writer therefore does not add a reason, mechanism, or recommendation the evidence does not show just to join two sentences. The rationale holds the measured range: in the twelve pre-2023 human-written papers, sentence-initial relation words ran from about one to six per 1,000 words. Agent drafts used almost none. The new first pair keeps a contrast word. Adapter and README one-liners say "additive-only openers".
- **RULE-12 asks for varied length and drops the hard cap.** Writers split a sentence when the reader must hold more than one open clause to reach its point. A long sentence that carries one connected thought can stay, and a draft with no long sentences reads clipped. The directive carries no percentage. Its rationale gives the measured figure, taken from LaTeX sources: about one sentence in six ran past 30 words in the twelve human-written papers (10% to 35% across the papers). A sentence about balancing an unavoidable long sentence with short neighbours is gone. The title drops its "(Split Sentences over 30 Words)" parenthetical, and the severity and enforcement lines follow the new reading.
- **RULE-B asks for varied clause joins.** A period, a comma with "and", "but", or "while", and a semicolon for two clauses too closely tied to stand apart all serve. Semicolons stay sparing, and turning every one into a period reads as clipped. The directive listed the semicolon as a replacement for the dash. Agent drafts used semicolons at or above the top of the human range, which runs from none to about four per 1,000 words in the twelve papers. The rule's source line now quotes the current AGENTS.md sentence ("prefer commas, colons, parentheses, or a new sentence; keep semicolons rare").
- **RULE-G limits title case to headings.** A full sentence used as a bold run-in paragraph lead takes sentence case, as it did in the human-written papers that had such leads. Title case stays for section and subsection headings.
- **RULE-03 no longer licenses prose stacks.** The last sentence told writers to keep numbers "that no display holds", which an agent can read as leave to write a stack of figures into a paragraph. It now keeps numbers that change the claim or that the reader acts on, and asks for a table (in an appendix if space is short) when several figures matter and no display holds them. Results paragraphs state the pattern early. A difference is reported in points or percent, not as a raw decimal the reader must convert, and text, tables, and captions share one unit and sign convention. After the figures, the text says what they mean for the reader (faster, cheaper, more accurate). A figure leaves a sentence only once a display holds it, and an appendix that serves as the record keeps exact values. In a first blind comparison, the maintainer preferred the 0.7.0 drafts in six of eight pairs and found the drafts written under an earlier version of this release wordier and harder to read. That version also asked for base rates, for how a varying result varies, and for no "tie", and those clauses were dropped. In a second blind comparison, of drafts written under the next wording, the maintainer preferred the 0.8.0 drafts in five of eight pairs and the 0.7.0 drafts in one, with two ties. The maintainer still objected to standard deviations and confidence-interval tests written into the prose of both versions. For uncertainty, the directive now asks for plain words ("within noise", "consistent across seeds") and leaves those statistics to the display. It also says not to call an inconclusive difference a gain even with a caveat attached, after bench drafts wrote "a 5% gain" next to "within noise" for a difference whose interval crossed zero. The fewest-numbers sentence now covers a conclusion as well as an abstract or introduction, after the maintainer found a conclusion crowded with figures. A third blind comparison, on eight fresh tasks with the final wording, preferred the 0.8.0 drafts in three pairs and the 0.7.0 drafts in four, with one tie. Two later changes followed a backtest of the 19 decided pairs from those three readings: the draft the maintainer chose carried fewer figures in 12 of 16 pairs that differed and fewer raw decimals in 8 of 9, while word count predicted little. The directive now asks writers not to restate a figure in a second form, to give a kept figure its meaning in the same sentence when it fits and only for the setting that produced it, and to state a tie or loss that bounds a summary with its size, in words or one rounded figure. A fourth blind comparison tested an earlier form of this change, without the size and setting clauses, against the third wording. The maintainer preferred it in five of nine pairs and the third wording in three, with one tie, and chose it for all three conclusions.
- **RULE-04 cuts needless sentences, and abstracts and conclusions get a shape.** The rule covered filler phrases only. It now also asks writers to cut a sentence that announces what the next one will do, restates the method or setup, or repeats a finding already given. An abstract states the problem, what makes it hard, the approach, and the main findings, defines only the terms those need, and cites no table or figure. A conclusion restates the main findings and their limits in a short paragraph. In the maintainer's blind readings, abstracts read as flat once they lost the challenge, and agent conclusions ran past 300 words. Five of six human-written abstracts checked in the comparison set move from the problem and its challenge to the approach and findings, and the human conclusions ran from about 100 to 460 words, with a median near 160. On 18 fresh tasks with three writers, drafts under the new RULE-03 and RULE-04 wording, set against the earlier 0.8.0 wording, ran about a quarter shorter in conclusions and about 15% shorter in results passages. They carried about a fifth fewer numbers and far fewer raw decimals, and no abstract cited a table (six had before). A keyword check found challenge language in the first three sentences of 17 of 21 abstracts, up from 12. Judged accuracy held: 31 unsupported claims against 33, 10 missing material qualifiers against 9, and no trap errors in either. The adapter one-liners follow.
- **The compact render drops the Severity Rubric and pays for the new text.** That rubric explains severity metadata the compact omits. Rationale-style sentences moved from nine directives (RULE-03, RULE-04, RULE-06, RULE-07, RULE-10, RULE-A, RULE-C, RULE-F, RULE-I) into their rationale paragraphs in `RULES.md`, RULE-G's list of heading surfaces moved to its scope line, and the compact introduction is two sentences. `docs/rule-pack-compact.md` is 22,620 bytes, the same as in 0.7.0. The adapter one-liners and the README follow the new wording, and the RULE-03 one-liner asks for points or percent rather than raw decimals, for uncertainty in plain words, and for standard deviations and intervals in tables. `claude-code.md` no longer names RULE-G "Sentence-case section headings". Rule text carries no version-history phrasing, the RULE-10 rationale no longer opens consecutive sentences with "Readers", and RULE-I drops its leftover "be deliberate".
- **The `style-review` skill treats findings as departures to consider.** Its reviser may keep a departure that reads well and judges the draft's overall pattern. Banned words are always replaced, and a RULE-H unsupported claim stays flagged. The reviser never adds a connective that asserts a cause, contrast, or sequence the source does not state. `references/rule-detectors.md` documents the two new detectors and their human baseline.

### Added

- **A test for the compact render.** `packages/pypi/tests/test_build_compact.py` pins that the compact omits the Severity Rubric and keeps the Escape Hatch while `RULES.md` keeps the rubric. It skips when `markdown-it-py` is absent.
- **Two hook-only detectors that read a whole document.** `_rule_12_profile` (`rule12Profile` in the npm package) reports under RULE-12 and replaces the per-sentence check in a hook. It makes three checks: a share of sentences over 30 words above 45% or below 6% in a complete LaTeX paper (against 10% to 35% in the human-written papers measured), six or more `, so` clause joins at 1.5 or more per 1,000 words in a LaTeX source (three times the human maximum; Markdown and text, where design notes use the join by design, are not read), and sentences over 80 words reported as one finding per document. The share check reads only a complete LaTeX document, which means `\begin{document}` and `\end{document}`, a paper class, and no `\input`, `\include` or `\subfile` of a body file left in the text. A section file, a fragment, a main file that pulls its sections in, and every Markdown or text file get no share check. `_rule_08_repeats` (`rule08Repeats`) reports under RULE-08 when the same caveat framing is stated three or more times outside a limitations section or an appendix. Each finding is worded as a departure from human-written prose, and a finding about the whole document begins with the words "Across the document,". Footnote bodies (LaTeX arguments and Markdown `[^label]:` definitions), acknowledgments, and YAML front matter are not counted as prose. Neither detector is registered in the CLI audit. `agent-style review` and the bench scope therefore do not change, and a hook such as the anywhere-agents guard imports them. The npm package carries both, with output identical to the Python package.
- **A human-baseline harness.** `scripts/human-baseline/measure.py` runs every detector and a set of prose measures over a corpus of LaTeX, Markdown, or text, and reports rates per document. `manifest.tsv` lists the 18 public arXiv papers behind this release's thresholds: twelve human-written papers from 2017 to 2022, and six papers on LLM agents from 2023 and 2024. No corpus is committed. Run it before changing a detector, to measure its false positives on human writing. None of the 18 papers triggers a share, clause-join, or repeated-caveat finding. Eight trigger the collapsed advisory for sentences over 80 words.

## [0.7.0] — 2026-10-17

Minor: the RULE-A and RULE-03 directives change, which `RELEASING.md` counts as a change to the ruleset contract, and the mechanical detectors gain a function. The rule count, the rule IDs, the CLI interface, and the published bench scope are unchanged.

### Changed

- **RULE-A covers tables, and RULE-03 covers captions.** Agent drafts of papers write paragraphs into table cells and put results, setup details, and caveats into captions. Across 91 tables from twelve pre-2023 human-written papers, no cell ran past six words; in 2026 agent-assisted drafts, one table in eight had a cell over fifteen. The drafts' 345 captions ran twice as long at the median as 169 human ones, and one in eight carried three or more precise figures. RULE-A's directive now applies its list test to tables: a cell holds a number, a label, or a short phrase. RULE-03's directive says a caption names what the display shows, the pattern to read from it, and what a reader needs to decode it, such as error bars. Repeated results, setup narration, and caveats go to the text or an appendix. Each rule gains a BAD → GOOD pair and a rationale paragraph, and the adapter one-liners follow.
- **The compact render pays for the new directive text.** RULE-12 now leads with the shorter runbook example, and the introduction to the field-observed rules is three short sentences. RULE-03's uncertainty example keeps one phrase, so `docs/rule-pack-compact.md` is 103 bytes smaller than in 0.6.0.

### Added

- **LaTeX checks in the hook-only detectors.** `_rule_03` in `detectors_mech.py` and `rule03` in `detectors_mech.js` gain three checks for LaTeX sources. The three-sentence figure run, which 0.6.0 checked only in an abstract or introduction, is now flagged anywhere in the body outside setup sections such as Methods. Paragraphs are flagged when four or more numbers use at least four decimal places and three significant digits; learning-rate grids such as 0.0001 stay quiet. Captions are flagged when they carry three or more precise figures, not counting legend values such as quantile lines or significance thresholds, or run past 100 words. A new `_rule_a` and `ruleA` flag a LaTeX table with a cell of more than 15 words or two sentences. All four skip appendices and supplements, now including files named `app-*`, `appx`, or `apx`, and the interval check honors the same names. `tblr` and `tabulary` join the display environments that RULE-03 skips. The measurement covered 169 files from the twelve human-written papers and 335 files of agent-assisted drafts. Human and draft files drew 0 and 53 body runs, 0 and 29 table findings, 2 and 14 precision findings, and 5 and 98 caption findings. Every human hit was a genuine instance, such as a paragraph quoting eight scores to four decimal places. Markdown is not read: long table cells are common in agent working notes, and flagging them would bury the paper findings. A hook that passes only an edited fragment cannot see the section or appendix around it. Hooks that import the detectors, such as the anywhere-agents guard from this release, run them as an advisory; `agent-style review` and the published bench scope are unchanged. The two engines give identical findings on the replayed corpus and on fuzzed LaTeX, apart from column offsets on astral characters. A pre-existing difference remains on non-ASCII digits and some control characters, in the figure counter that checks A, B, and C share.

## [0.6.0] — 2026-10-16

Minor: the RULE-03 directive changes, which `RELEASING.md` counts as a change to the ruleset contract, and the mechanical detectors gain a function. The rule count, the rule IDs, the CLI interface, and the published bench scope are unchanged.

### Changed

- **RULE-03 asks for rounded magnitudes and no stacked figures.** v0.5.0 told agents to keep uncertainty beside an estimate, and agents read that as an interval for every finding. In a 2026 blind comparison, three agents wrote abstracts for the same two conference drafts. On each draft, the two versions the author ranked lower put an interval beside nearly every finding and stacked precise figures across consecutive sentences. The directive now asks for each magnitude rounded to the precision the claim needs. It rules out precise figures in consecutive sentences and gives an abstract or introduction the fewest numbers of any section, with no intervals or p-values. One pair is new, a paper abstract on a synthetic caching result, and the rationale gains a paragraph on the comparison. The adapter one-liners in the eight adapters that carry directives say the same in one clause.

### Added

- **A mechanical check for RULE-03.** `_rule_03` in `detectors_mech.py` and `rule03` in `detectors_mech.js` give the same findings on the replayed corpus, apart from known Unicode differences in case matching and column offsets. Each counts precise figures: decimals and percentages, never integer counts, versions, dates, section or table numbers, model names, identifiers, or anything in code, tables, or equations. Markdown tables count with or without outer pipes, and code fences close only as CommonMark closes them. In body prose, it flags a sentence that writes a numeric interval, and asks the author to keep in words what the reader needs to judge the claim. Appendices and supplements are exempt from that check, because they are where such detail belongs. Within an abstract or introduction, it also flags three consecutive sentences with two or more figures each, or one sentence with five or more. A file name, an abstract environment, an Introduction or Abstract section, or a heading of that name marks the region, so the function takes an optional `file_path`. On the six generated abstracts of the comparison, it flags the four the author ranked lower and passes the other two. Both abstracts already in the drafts pass as well. Across the ordinary documentation of this repository and its two sibling configuration repositories, it reports nothing outside two saved review notes that quote intervals. `agent-style review` does not run it, with or without `--mechanical-only`, because the v0.3.0 bench replay pins the mechanical rule set. Hooks that import the detectors, such as the anywhere-agents guard, run it as an advisory, and the style-review detector matrix says so. `packages/pypi/tests/test_rule03_figures.py` and the npm review tests cover it with synthetic text only.

## [0.5.0] — 2026-10-06

Minor: the RULE-03 and RULE-08 directives now ask for calibration in both directions, which `RELEASING.md` counts as a change to the ruleset contract. The rule count, the rule IDs, the adapters' structure and the CLI interface are unchanged. Agent drafts of papers and proposals were attaching a caveat to good results and transcribing tables into prose, and the rule pack's own examples modeled both habits.

### Changed

- **RULE-08 names the structural form of underclaiming.** The directive already ruled out overclaiming and reflexive weasel wording. It now also names the caveat tail: a "however", a future-work aside, or a note on what the work does not establish, attached to a result by habit. A second paragraph gives the test for any qualifier: it stays, inside the claim, when it changes what the evidence supports or what the reader should conclude. Ties, losses, differences within noise, and the weakest cases are findings, reported next to the claims they bound and in any summary of the results. Qualifiers that add nothing are cut. General limitations of the study design go to the venue's Limitations section. The first pair, which every compact consumer loads, used to end its GOOD sentence on a future-work parenthetical. Its BAD now shows hype and a caveat tail together, and its GOOD drops the tail. Three pairs are new: negative framing, a mixed result stated as a finding, and a proposal limit turned into an aim. Two GOOD sentences were corrected so they no longer turn "no measurable speedup" or "within one standard deviation" into stronger claims. The rationale gains a paragraph on the measured habit and on the Limitations sections that NeurIPS and ACL Rolling Review provide.
- **RULE-03 gains its counterweight.** Concrete means naming the specific finding, and a table transcribed into a sentence buries it. When a display holds the results, prose states the pattern with the magnitude a reader needs and points to the display. Uncertainty that changes the interpretation goes beside the estimate, and an inconclusive difference is not called a gain. Numbers that change the claim, that the reader acts on, or that no display holds stay. Its first GOOD sentence carried five numbers and now carries two. Three pairs are new. The enforcement line no longer names a "claim-without-number heuristic", which never existed.
- **RULE-H examples state evidence without stacking scores.** The directive asks for "the magnitude or a table pointer" where it asked for "number". Four GOOD sentences were rewritten; the SimCLR example now names the four-times-wider ResNet-50 behind the 76.5% result, a condition the old sentence left out.
- **Semantic judge prompts** in `skills/style-review/references/rule-detectors.md` and its two package copies now cover both directions. RULE-08 asks about both, RULE-03 asks about transcribed tables, and RULE-H accepts a table pointer as checkable evidence. The old prompts asked for claims "without a metric/name/date", which rewarded inline numbers.
- **Adapter one-liners** for RULE-03 and RULE-08 in the eight adapters that carry directives say the same in one clause each.

### Added

- **Habit bench** (`scripts/bench/habit/`): a before-and-after check of the compact pack on paper and proposal tasks, with a blind cross-family judge and a per-writer, per-task safety gate against overclaiming. The first wording of this release failed that gate: one writer dropped qualifiers that changed the claim. Two revisions followed, each checked on a new held-out task. On the release wording, reflexive qualifiers fall from 3 to 0, no judged trap error appears, and B wins both pairwise questions (62% and 69%). The strict per-cell safety criterion still fails on three minor error patterns, and numbers per sentence fall 13% against a 25% target. Results and evidence: [`docs/bench-habit-v0.5.0.md`](docs/bench-habit-v0.5.0.md). The maintainer shipped this release on 2026-10-06 as a field trial; section 8 of that report records the decision.

## [0.4.2] — 2026-08-14

Patch. The ruleset, the adapters and the CLI interface are unchanged. This release restores deterministic review on Python 3.9 through 3.11 and aligns the declared Python floor with the versions CI exercises.

### Fixed

- **Deterministic review failed below Python 3.12.** `detectors_mech.py` built RULE-B's message with a backslash escape inside an f-string expression, which Python first accepts in 3.12 under PEP 701. On 3.8 through 3.11, importing that detector raised `SyntaxError`. The detector loads lazily, when `audit()` or `compare()` runs, so `agent-style review`, `style-review`, and a direct import of `agent_style.review.detectors_mech` failed. Top-level `import agent_style`, `import agent_style.review`, and CLI commands that do not reach review, such as `list-tools`, kept working. That partial surface is what made the failure quiet: `pip install agent-style` succeeded, the package imported, and the break arrived only on the first review. The em-dash literal now lives in a module constant, leaving the reported `em` and `en` text unchanged.

- **Python 3.9 could not read the bundled ruleset directly.** `loader.py` asked for `importlib.resources.files("agent_style.data")`, and `agent_style/data/` ships no `__init__.py`, so it is a namespace package. Python 3.9's `files()` passes that package's `None` origin to `pathlib.Path` and raises `TypeError: expected str, bytes or os.PathLike object, not NoneType`. Python 3.10 adds a namespace resource reader and handles the case.

  Scope, stated carefully. In the pre-tag `python-floor` job the checkout had no project-local override and no installed `agent-style` console script, and the `v0.4.2` raw URL did not exist yet. All four resolution steps failed there, `load_rules()` raised `RulesLoadError`, and deterministic review stayed unusable on 3.9 even after the detector repair. An installed release could instead recover through the `agent-style rules` subcommand when it was on `PATH`, or through the version-pinned raw URL when the network was reachable. The subcommand works offline when installed on `PATH`; only the raw URL requires network access. Neither fallback makes direct bundle loading correct.

  The call is now anchored on `agent_style`, a regular package on every supported version, and navigates to `data` from there. The defect had been present since deterministic review arrived in v0.2.0, across eight package-bearing releases. The new floor job found it on its first run: 3.10 and 3.11 passed and 3.9 did not.

- **Nothing in this repository ran the Python code on the versions the package advertised.** Every job in every workflow pinned `python-version: '3.12'` while `requires-python` said `>=3.8`, so the claim was asserted and never checked. That is why a defect this plain survived a release: the suite is thorough and only ever ran on the one version whose parser accepts the syntax. A `python-floor` job now runs on 3.9, 3.10 and 3.11. It imports every shipped module as a separate step before the suite. A `SyntaxError` at import is the failure it exists to catch, and that diagnosis should not arrive buried in a collection error.

  The bug was found downstream rather than here, by `anywhere-agents`, whose own matrix covers 3.9 through 3.13 and which had just started installing this package in CI. A consumer's matrix caught what the producer's did not.

### Changed

- **`requires-python` is now `>=3.9`, and the 3.8 classifier is dropped.** The floor names the oldest version CI runs. Declaring 3.8 while testing only 3.12 is the same defect as the one above, one layer up, and 3.8 reached end of life in October 2024. This does end the partial 3.8 support that 0.4.1 still had: top-level imports and the CLI paths that never reach review ran there, while deterministic review did not. Under `RELEASING.md`'s policy this is a patch. The ruleset, adapter and CLI interface contracts are untouched, and the change corrects an incomplete packaging claim.

## [0.4.1] — 2026-08-13

Patch rather than minor, under a rule this release writes down. RULE-12 reports differently for the same Markdown input in both engines, changing 15 of the 160 preserved drafts, so anyone comparing violation counts across versions will see movement. Moved counts alone no longer decide the version number. No published contract changed here: RULE-12's directive is untouched, and the detector was corrected to match it. Version 0.4.0 took the same class of repair as grounds for a minor bump, in release prose rather than as policy. `RELEASING.md` now carries a rule that supersedes that judgment, so the next one is not a fresh argument. The corrected benchmark figure is labelled as v0.4.1 scoring; the hero figure keeps its original v0.3.0 scoring label.

### Fixed

- **`real-agent-smoke` asserted a style-review handshake that v0.4.0 had already replaced.** The skill's self-verification sentence changed from `21 rules` to `21-row scorecard, 19 rules checked`, and both assertions in the `style-review-skill` job kept requiring the old substring. The static check passed regardless, because `21 rules` still appears in the skill's frontmatter description, so the only step that noticed was the live-model probe, and it noticed on the v0.4.0 tag with the package already on PyPI. Both token lists now name the current handshake. The four adapter jobs keep `21 rules` on purpose: an adapter loads all 21 rules, while the skill emits 21 scorecard rows and checks 19 of them.
- **The handshake is pinned at push time instead of at release time.** `real-agent-smoke` runs on `release.published` and manual dispatch only, so nothing could catch this drift until the release existed. `packages/pypi/tests/test_handshake_parity.py` now asserts that the sentence reads identically in `skills/style-review/SKILL.md`, both packaged copies and the README, and that every literal the workflow asserts is a substring of that sentence. Deleting the workflow guard fails the test too. Verified against three seeded regressions: the pre-0.4.0 token list, a drifted npm copy, and a removed assertion.
- **RULE-12 now sees a sentence boundary through Markdown markup.** The splitter required the next sentence to open with a capital, a quote, `(` or `[`. Prose opens sentences with an inline code span or with emphasis constantly, and none of those characters was in the class. Two ordinary 20-word sentences were therefore counted as one 40-word violation. Markup on the closing side hid boundaries too. A sentence ending `.**` puts the emphasis between the period and the whitespace, so the lookbehind never saw the period. A bolded lead was read as running into the sentence after it. Both engines now admit `` ` ``, `*` and `_` as openers, and absorb an optional closing `**`, `__`, `*`, `_` or `` ` `` before the whitespace. Every markup opener sits in the guarded branch, which is the part that is easy to get wrong. The guards cover the abbreviations that routinely precede a formatted value: `e.g.`, `i.e.`, `vs.`, `cf.` and `Fig.`. Unguarded, each of those reads as a sentence end and silently suppresses a genuine violation, trading a visible false positive for an invisible false negative. `etc.` is deliberately left unguarded, because at the end of a sentence it is a real boundary. Fixtures mirror across both engines and run each abbreviation against each of the four markup forms, because a guard applied to one delimiter looks correct until another is tried.
- **The bench figures are restated for that repair.** RULE-12 falls from 338 hits to 307 across the 160 preserved drafts, changing 15 of them. `docs/bench-0.3.0-rescored.md` was regenerated with `--verify-repeat`, so two replay passes had to agree before it was written. The pooled corrected delta moves from -126 to -119, against an unchanged original of -133. Per runner the corrected deltas become -41 for Claude (88 → 47), -22 for Codex (48 → 26), -56 for Gemini (65 → 9), and 0 for the Copilot control (52 → 52). That control now reports no change at all rather than +1. `docs/bench.png` was regenerated and the README restated to match, including the Gemini panel, where RULE-05 displaces RULE-I among the four largest movers. The three-rule concentration is 112 of 119, which is still 94%.

- **The re-scored report now says what produced it, and refuses inputs it cannot name.** It carried no engine identity at all, so a reader could not tell which detectors computed a published number. It now records the engine version, a SHA-256 manifest over all 19 scoring-source paths, and a second SHA-256 over the four published scorecards the original column is read from. The manifest is glob-derived from `agent_style/**/*.py` rather than hand-listed, so a new module joins it without anyone remembering to. An earlier attempt hashed `detectors_mech.py` alone, which left the identity byte-identical after edits to `primitive.py`, where the mechanical-bucket classification lives. Each entry is length-prefixed by path and by content, so no rename can collide with a content change. Content is normalised to LF first. `.gitattributes` gives every fresh clone LF, but an older working tree can still hold CRLF that `git diff` reports as no change. Hashing raw bytes therefore made the published identity depend on which working tree ran the replay. Recomputing the digest from the release tree, which `RELEASING.md` requires before tagging, would then have disagreed with the report for no substantive reason. Line endings cannot move a score in either engine, so nothing is given up by normalising. ## [0.4.0] — 2026-08-11

Minor rather than patch: six detector-backed rules (RULE-05, RULE-A, RULE-B, RULE-D, RULE-G and RULE-I) now report differently for the same input, and the Node engine's file-reading semantics changed. RULE-A is structural; the other five are mechanical. Any consumer comparing violation counts across versions will see movement.

### Fixed

- **RULE-05 no longer suppresses a cliché attached by a hyphen.** The boundary class was `[A-Za-z0-9_-]`, which treated a following hyphen as part of the word, so `paradigm shift-based` and `state-of-the-art-system` went unreported. Both boundaries are now `[A-Za-z0-9_]`. The exclusions the rule intends are unaffected: `a novelty-detection` and `novel methodology` still do not fire, because a word character, unlike a hyphen, does end the match.
- **RULE-A judges list meaning instead of list syntax.** The detector exempted every ordered list, so a numbered causal chain (`1. Because retrieval is hard` / `2. Therefore we add reranking`) was invisible while the same text with dashes fired. RULE-A's directive never privileges a marker. The exemption is gone, and shortness alone is no longer evidence either, because the directive names checklist steps as a genuine list. A list of three or more items is flagged when it carries a **strong** signal at any item length (an item opening with a coordinator, subordinator or relative pronoun, or at least two items leading with `Per` while at least two do not) or a **weak** one when every item is also short (two or more items repeating a subject-and-copula opening). Imperative checklists, preposition-led enumerations such as `For Linux, use apt`, nested child lists, and short independent labels stay clean.
- **RULE-B now exempts paired-name en dashes.** Numeric ranges were already exempt, but v0.3.6 still reported directive-approved forms such as `Cauchy–Schwarz`. The detector now exempts an en dash between two adjacent capitalized tokens. That test is capitalization adjacency rather than name recognition, which is recorded in Notes.
- **RULE-D now applies one transition-opener allowance per detected Markdown block.** v0.3.6 reported every recognized line-initial opener and recognized only four of the six directive-listed forms. The detector now recognizes all six at sentence starts, permits the first within each block, and resets at blank lines, headings, leading-pipe table rows, thematic breaks, list items, ordinary fences, and markup-only lines. The non-fence boundaries are also recognized after blockquote markers; a fence inside a blockquote is a known limitation.
- **RULE-G now follows the directive's title-case scope.** It exempts conservatively recognized sentence-style headings and spelling-sensitive code, link, HTML, version, identifier, path, and filename tokens; it uses RULE-G's stated minor-word set; and it checks title-case boundaries inside hyphenated compounds. The finite-auxiliary heuristic and the link-label removal remain recorded limitations.
- **RULE-I recognises the full closed `'d` host set.** `why'd`, `there'd`, `where'd` and `how'd` were missing.
- **`agent_style.list_tools()` no longer raises `KeyError: 'target_path'`.** A skill-type tool installs into surfaces listed under `target_groups`, and the registry schema forbids it a top-level `target_path`, but `list_tools()` indexed that key unconditionally. The public API has therefore raised for every caller since v0.2.0, when `style-review` became the first skill. It now reports `None` for such a tool rather than inventing a path. The Node registry never had this defect. Found by a release rehearsal installing the built package from TestPyPI and calling the API, which is the only check that exercises the shipped artifact rather than the source tree.
- **Python and Node now agree on any file, whatever its line endings.** Python reads with `open(path, encoding="utf-8")`, whose default `newline=None` applies universal-newline translation, so every Python detector has always seen LF. `fs.readFileSync` does not translate. The Node reader now normalises, which is the actual cause of a divergence that had been visible as a Node-only defect: on a CRLF file, a Windows reader silently got wrong RULE-A results. The detectors were additionally given a shared CR/LF/CRLF line model so a direct-text caller is correct too, and RULE-05 now locates matches with that model rather than counting `\n`.
- **`scripts/bump-version.py` no longer rewrites release history.** `RELEASING.md` narrates past releases by version number and was not on the skip list, so a bump silently renamed the release that a recorded packaging incident happened in.

### Added

- **`docs/bench-0.3.0-rescored.md`.** The 160 committed drafts re-scored with the repaired detectors, giving the original and corrected value side by side per rule and per runner. Repairing the instrument moves the pooled delta from -133 to -126.
- **`scripts/bench/rescore.py` and a `--drafts PATH` replay mode in `scripts/bench/run.sh`.** Replay re-scores saved drafts without calling any model and reuses the original scoring invocation, so old and new numbers are comparable by construction. It refuses to publish against modified drafts and records the drafts tree hash in the report. An empty `--drafts` value fails closed rather than falling through to generation.
- **Exact-object regression tests in both runtimes.** The fixture tests compared per-rule counts only, so a false positive and a false negative for the same rule cancelled out. The suites now assert complete violation objects, including `excerpt` and `detail`, and cover RULE-05 under LF, CRLF and bare CR through both the file path and the detector directly. Python goes from 16 tests to 46, Node from 15 to 45.
- **A `unit-tests` CI job** on `ubuntu-latest` and `windows-latest` across Node 18 and 24, and a `.gitattributes` that normalises line endings in the repository. The Node RULE-A defect above reproduced only on CRLF checkouts, which no job ran.

### Changed

- **Every benchmark scorecard heading now says `mechanical only; structural and semantic excluded`.** The harness has always passed `--mechanical-only`, so the structural detectors never contributed to a published number, but the generated heading claimed `mechanical + structural` in all eleven documents.
- **README "Does It Work?" states the measurement's scope.** The published percentages cover 7 of the 21 rules. Three of those seven (sentence length, dashes, jargon list) account for a net 120 of the pooled 133-violation reduction under the original scoring, or 90%; under the corrected scoring the same all-arm calculation is 119 of 126, or 94%. Both critical-severity rules, RULE-01 and RULE-H, are measured by no bench in this repository. The GitHub Copilot arm is reported as a noise control rather than a fourth result, because `copilot -p` showed no measurable effect from the instruction file in the April 2026 setup; that archived result is version- and setup-specific and does not describe present-day behavior.
- **`agent_style_version` in both bundled `tools.json` files** was still `0.2.0` while the three version files were current. The field is parsed but unused, so no behavior changes.

### Notes

- Known and tracked, not fixed here: the Python CLI emits CRLF on Windows, breaking the byte-identical canonical JSON claim; `\w` is Unicode-aware in Python and ASCII-only in JavaScript, so RULE-12 word counts diverge on accented prose and RULE-I misses the typographic apostrophe; RULE-G treats a finite auxiliary anywhere as proof of a sentence-style heading; RULE-B's paired-name test is capitalization adjacency; RULE-A does not see lists inside blockquotes or `1)` ordered markers; and RULE-12's sentence splitter does not break before Markdown emphasis.

## [0.2.0] — 2026-04-20

### Added

- **`style-review` skill: second-pass review loop** complementing the generation-time ruleset. The existing `enable <tool>` adapters remain soft enforcement (the model sees the rules and is asked to follow them, but can ignore them). The new skill is the opt-in post-hoc pass: the user asks for it explicitly, the skill audits the prose against the same 21 rules, and on confirmation produces a revised draft at `FILE.reviewed.md` without touching the source. Designed around the observation that prompt-loaded rules nudge the model but cannot override training-prior token distributions, and that adding more examples has diminishing returns — the complement is a review loop the user invokes after the first draft is written.

- **`agent-style review <file>` CLI subcommand** in both the pip and npm packages. Deterministic mechanical + structural detection from the plain CLI (works in any pip / npm / curl / clone context). Flags:
  - `--audit-only`: emit canonical JSON with per-rule violation counts and excerpts; no polish, no ask.
  - `--mechanical-only`: strictest deterministic subset; used by `scripts/verify-install.sh --cli-parity` as the byte-identity oracle.
  - `--compare A B`: A/B audit with per-rule delta (used for benchmarking).
  - `--polish`: requires a skill host (Claude Code / Anthropic Skills); errors with an actionable message from the plain CLI.
  - No new runtime dependencies in either ecosystem. Semantic rules (RULE-01, 03, 04, 08, 11, F, H) emit `status: "skipped"` from the plain CLI and are handled by the skill host.

- **Ten deterministic detectors** covering the mechanical + structural subset of the 21-rule matrix:
  - Mechanical: RULE-B (em/en-dash casual use), RULE-D (transition openers `Additionally` / `Furthermore` / `Moreover` / `In addition`), RULE-G (heading title case per RULE-G's own convention), RULE-I (contractions in formal prose), RULE-12 (sentences >30 words), RULE-05 (cliché phrase list from Orwell 1946 + RULES.md BAD examples), RULE-06 (45-word banned AI-tell list + Orwell Rule 5 jargon).
  - Structural: RULE-A (bullet overuse: ≤2 items or all items ≤8 words), RULE-C (≥2 of 3 consecutive sentences share opening token), RULE-E (paragraph closers restating the topic).
  - RULE-02, 07, 09, 10 structural detectors stubbed as skipped in v0.2.0 (deferred to v0.3.0).

- **New `skill-with-references` install mode** in `tools.json`. `agent-style enable style-review` auto-detects which skill-capable surfaces are active in the project (Claude Code, Anthropic Skills, detected via existing `.agent-style/` or `.claude/skills/` files) and writes `SKILL.md` + `references/*` for each, deduplicated by target path. For tools without a skills directory (Codex, Copilot, Aider, Cursor), it prints a manual-step message pointing at the CLI fallback. No-surface case exits with `enabled: false, manual_step_required: true` and a message. Backward-compatible schema extension (`schema_version` stays at 1).

- **Manifest-based safe disable**. `agent-style enable style-review` writes `.agent-style/skills/style-review/manifest.json` listing every path + sha256 it created. `disable` reads the manifest and removes only files whose current hash still matches; user-edited files are skipped and reported as `drifted`; empty directories are cleaned up; non-empty directories are left in place with a report. No blind recursive deletes.

- **Bundled skill definition** at `skills/style-review/` (synced into both `packages/pypi/agent_style/data/skills/style-review/` and `packages/npm/data/skills/style-review/`):
  - `SKILL.md` with invocation contract, workflow, polish invariants, and self-verification probe.
  - `references/rule-detectors.md` — full 21-row detector matrix (bucket, approach, threshold, fixture).
  - `references/revision-prompt.md` — polish-pass template with hard no-new-facts invariants (no metrics / citations / links / code behavior not in source; preserve Markdown structure; preserve meaning; preserve length budget).
  - `references/fixture-prose/` — 5 fixtures (`mech-violations.md`, `struct-violations.md`, `mixed.md`, `messy-real-world.md`, `clean-control.md`) each with `*.expected.json` documenting per-rule violation counts. Fixtures drive the unit-test suite in both ecosystems.

- **Unit tests** (new):
  - Python: `packages/pypi/tests/test_review_fixtures.py` — 9 tests covering every fixture, plus regression guards (clean-control produces zero; fenced `leverages` not flagged; semantic-skipped contract; mechanical-only purity).
  - Node: `packages/npm/test/review.test.js` — same 9 tests using `node --test` (stdlib; zero new deps). Fixtures and expected counts are byte-for-byte identical between Python and Node.

- **Sanity benchmark** (Phase 3 from PLAN):
  - `scripts/bench/tasks.md`: 10 concrete prose tasks (5 PR descriptions, 3 design-doc sections, 2 commit messages).
  - `scripts/bench/run.sh`: bash runner that generates baseline vs treatment drafts via `claude -p`, scores each with `agent-style review --mechanical-only --audit-only`, and emits a Markdown scorecard at `docs/bench-<version>.md`.

## [0.1.1] — 2026-04-19

### Fixed

- **npm CLI**: `agent-style rules` on Linux and macOS truncated piped output at the 64 KiB kernel pipe-buffer boundary. Root cause: `process.stdout.write()` returned `false` when the buffer filled and `process.exit()` ran before the queued bytes drained, so `agent-style rules | less` or `agent-style rules | wc -c` lost the last ~25 KiB of `RULES.md`. Fix lives in `packages/npm/bin/agent-style.js`: wait for `stdout` and `stderr` to drain via zero-byte writes with drain callbacks before calling `process.exit`, and swallow `EPIPE` from consumers that close the pipe early (for example `agent-style rules | head -3`). Windows (non-POSIX pipe semantics) and the Python CLI are unaffected by the original bug. Regression covered by `scripts/verify-fresh-install.py` on Linux aarch64.

### Changed

- Bundled `RULES.md` content refresh (no structural change; all 21 rule bodies still line up with the published rule IDs):
  - Stale "Phase 1b draft" banner at the top of the canonical rule file removed.
  - Author voice normalized from "the maintainer" to "I / my / me" across field-observed source metadata and the descriptive intro, so the ruleset reads as a personal project. Field-observed source lines now say "My observation of LLM output across dozens of writing projects and code releases, 2022 – 2026" in place of the prior category enumeration.
