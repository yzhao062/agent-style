Graph anomaly detection flags nodes whose behavior deviates from the rest of a graph, such as fraudulent accounts or illicit transactions. Existing methods either need manually labeled anomalies or degrade on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that requires no anomaly labels. GraphGuard pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective that contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries. We compare it with GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on four datasets: Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD. Table 1 reports area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP), averaged over 5 seeds. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, GraphGuard raises AUC by 0.059 to 0.083 and AP by 0.072 to 0.087 over SubgraphAnomaly. Seed standard deviations are at most 0.006, well below these gains. GraphGuard also outperforms GCN on all four datasets, by 0.079 to 0.122 AUC. On Cora-OOD, GraphGuard ties SubgraphAnomaly: AUC is 0.891 against 0.888 and AP is 0.835 against 0.831, differences within noise.
