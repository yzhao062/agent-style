### Preliminary Results

We piloted FedVerify on a 100-node heterogeneous edge testbed in which 20 nodes (20%) were Byzantine clients that sent poisoned model updates. Client data followed a non-IID Dirichlet split with alpha = 0.2, which gives each client a highly skewed label distribution. We compared FedVerify with FedAvg without any defense (McMahan et al., 2017), Krum (Blanchard et al., 2017), and FL-Trust (Cao et al., 2021). Table 4 reports rounds and wall-clock time to reach 80% accuracy, final accuracy, and communication per round.

Without a defense, FedAvg never reaches 80% accuracy and ends at 34.2%, 51.2 points below FedVerify. FedVerify reaches 80% accuracy in 115 rounds, against 210 for FL-Trust and 340 for Krum, which is 45% fewer than FL-Trust and 66% fewer than Krum. Wall-clock time to 80% accuracy drops by 50% against FL-Trust (5.6 versus 11.2 hours) and by 62% against Krum (5.6 versus 14.8 hours). The speedup comes from the smaller round count. An average FedVerify round takes 2.9 minutes (wall-clock time divided by rounds), between Krum at 2.6 and FL-Trust at 3.2.

FedVerify ends at 85.4% accuracy, 2.3 points above FL-Trust (83.1%) and 4.4 points above Krum (81.0%). Table 4 gives no confidence intervals, so we treat these margins as suggestive until repeated runs supply them. Per-round communication is higher for FedVerify than for FedAvg and Krum: 14.1 MB against 12.4 MB, an increase of 1.7 MB (14%). FL-Trust sends 18.2 MB per round, 4.1 MB (23%) more than FedVerify. Cumulative traffic to reach 80% accuracy, computed as rounds times per-round volume, is about 1.6 GB for FedVerify, against 3.8 GB for FL-Trust and 4.2 GB for Krum.
