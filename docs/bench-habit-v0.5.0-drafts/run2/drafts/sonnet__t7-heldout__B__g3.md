TrafficCast's accuracy gain over the baselines shrinks as the sensor count falls (Table 7). On Metro-A (2,100 sensors) and Metro-B (1,450), TrafficCast cuts the 60-minute mean absolute error (MAE) of the strongest baseline, GraphWaveNet, by about 11%. The reductions are 0.41 km/h on Metro-A (3.53 to 3.12) and 0.35 km/h on Metro-B (3.30 to 2.95), far above the seed standard deviations of 0.03 to 0.05. On Coastal (640 sensors), TrafficCast and GraphWaveNet tie: the 0.02 km/h difference (2.89 against 2.91) lies within the seed standard deviations of 0.05 to 0.06. Rural-Net (180 sensors) is the one network where TrafficCast loses to both neural baselines. Its MAE of 3.21 km/h is 0.19 above GraphWaveNet (3.02) and 0.16 above DCRNN (3.05), gaps larger than the seed standard deviations of 0.08 to 0.10. TrafficCast also costs more at inference: 41 ms per batch against 29 ms for GraphWaveNet, a 41% increase.
