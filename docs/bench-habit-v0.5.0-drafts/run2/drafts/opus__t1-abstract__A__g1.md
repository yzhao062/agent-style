Graph anomaly detection identifies nodes whose structure or attributes deviate from the rest of a graph, such as fraudulent accounts in a transaction network. Supervised detectors need manually labeled anomalies, which are scarce and costly to collect. Existing unsupervised detectors avoid labels, but their accuracy degrades on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective: the encoder learns to separate embeddings of subgraphs sampled across community boundaries. Averaged over five seeds, GraphGuard outperforms SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger of two baselines, on three of four benchmarks. AUC rises by 0.083 on Reddit-B (0.781 to 0.864), 0.081 on Twitch-EN, and 0.059 on Elliptic; average precision (AP) rises by 0.072 to 0.087 on the same datasets. On Cora-OOD, GraphGuard leads SubgraphAnomaly by only 0.003 AUC and 0.004 AP, a margin we treat as a tie given seed standard deviations of up to 0.006. Both methods exceed the GCN baseline on that dataset by at least 0.076 AUC.
