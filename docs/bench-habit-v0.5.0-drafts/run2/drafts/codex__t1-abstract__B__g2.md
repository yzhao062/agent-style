Graph anomaly detection aims to identify unusual patterns in relational data. Existing methods either require manual labels or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised graph anomaly detection method that incorporates community boundaries into contrastive pretraining. Its objective contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries, using differences between communities as a learning signal. This design targets anomaly detection in densely connected communities without requiring manual anomaly labels. We evaluate GraphGuard against GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD, reporting means over five seeds. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, GraphGuard improves over SubgraphAnomaly by 0.059 to 0.083 in area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and 0.072 to 0.087 in average precision (AP). On Cora-OOD, the methods perform comparably, with GraphGuard ahead by 0.003 AUC and 0.004 AP. GraphGuard also exceeds GCN on all four datasets in both metrics.
