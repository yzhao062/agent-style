Graph anomaly detection finds nodes whose behavior deviates from the rest of a graph, such as fraudulent accounts in a transaction network or bots on a social platform. Existing methods either require manual anomaly labels, which are costly to collect, or degrade on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that pretrains its encoder with a contrastive objective contrasting subgraphs across community boundaries. Four datasets (Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD) test GraphGuard against a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly, with results averaged over 5 seeds. Against SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger baseline, GraphGuard gains 5.9 to 8.3 points of area under the ROC curve (AUC) on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic. Average precision (AP) rises by 7.2 to 8.7 points on the same datasets. Cora-OOD is a tie: GraphGuard reaches AUC 0.891 versus 0.888 and AP 0.835 versus 0.831, within seed noise (standard deviations at most 0.006).
