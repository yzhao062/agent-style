TrafficCast achieves the lowest 60-minute MAE on the two largest networks, Metro-A (3.12 km/h) and Metro-B (2.95 km/h). It improves on GraphWaveNet, the strongest baseline, by 0.41 km/h (11.6%) and 0.35 km/h (10.6%), and each gap exceeds eight times the seed standard deviation (Table 7). On Coastal (640 sensors), the 0.02 km/h difference between TrafficCast (2.89) and GraphWaveNet (2.91) falls below the seed standard deviation of 0.05 to 0.06, so we treat the two as tied. Rural-Net (180 sensors) is the exception: TrafficCast reaches 3.21 km/h, which is 0.19 km/h worse than GraphWaveNet (3.02) and 0.16 km/h worse than DCRNN (3.05). Across the four networks, the gain over GraphWaveNet shrinks as sensor count falls and reverses at 180 sensors. This pattern suggests that TrafficCast benefits from larger graphs, but four networks cannot separate graph size from the other differences among them. TrafficCast also costs more at inference, at 41 ms per batch versus 29 ms for GraphWaveNet (41% slower).
