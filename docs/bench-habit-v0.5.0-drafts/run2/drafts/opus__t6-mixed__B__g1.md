Table 6 reports mean accuracy and standard deviation over 10 runs. Across the three larger datasets, augmentation changes accuracy by at most 0.5 points in either direction. On Adult, all three methods lie within 0.2 points of each other, inside one standard deviation. Credit-default shows a gain of about half a point for both SMOTE and TabMix, roughly one standard deviation; the two methods sit 0.1 points apart. Covertype is the one dataset where augmentation hurts: SMOTE loses 0.5 points, more than twice its standard deviation, and TabMix loses 0.2. The large effect appears on Churn-small, which has only 2,000 rows. There, TabMix reaches 81.7%, which is 5.3 points above no augmentation and 3.7 points above SMOTE; both margins exceed three standard deviations. Because Churn-small is the only small dataset in our evaluation, it offers a single data point for the hypothesis that TabMix helps most when training data is scarce.
