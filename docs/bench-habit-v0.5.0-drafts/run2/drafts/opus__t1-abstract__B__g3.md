Graph anomaly detection identifies nodes or subgraphs whose attributes or connections deviate from the rest of a graph, such as illicit transactions in a payment network. Current detectors either require manually labeled anomalies or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that trains without anomaly labels. GraphGuard pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective, which pulls the representations of matched inputs together and pushes mismatched inputs apart. The objective contrasts subgraphs drawn across community boundaries. We evaluate on four benchmarks with two metrics, area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP), each averaged over five seeds. GraphGuard outperforms a graph convolutional network (GCN) baseline on all four benchmarks, by 8 to 12 AUC points. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, it also beats the stronger baseline, SubgraphAnomaly, by 6 to 8 AUC points and 7 to 9 AP points. Cora-OOD is a tie: with seed standard deviations at or below 0.6 points, GraphGuard's margins of 0.3 AUC and 0.4 AP points fall within noise.
