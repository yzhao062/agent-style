Graph anomaly detection finds entities in a graph whose attributes or connections deviate from normal patterns, such as fraudulent accounts in a transaction network. Current detectors either require manually labeled anomalies, which are expensive to collect, or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. In such graphs, tightly connected groups of normal nodes can mask anomalous ones. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that needs no anomaly labels. It pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective, a loss that shapes representations by comparing pairs of samples. GraphGuard draws each pair from subgraphs on opposite sides of a community boundary, so pretraining focuses on the structure that degrades current detectors. Across four benchmarks and five seeds, GraphGuard outperforms both baselines, GCN and SubgraphAnomaly, on three datasets. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, it beats SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger baseline, by 0.059 to 0.083 AUC and 0.072 to 0.087 average precision (AP). Cora-OOD is the exception: the two methods tie, and their gaps of 0.003 AUC and 0.004 AP fall within seed-to-seed variation.
