Graph anomaly detection identifies nodes whose attributes or connections deviate from the rest of a graph, such as fraudulent accounts in a transaction network. Current detectors either depend on manually labeled anomalies or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that requires no anomaly labels. GraphGuard pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective, which teaches the encoder to map similar inputs close together and dissimilar inputs far apart. It contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries, targeting the dense-community graphs where existing label-free detectors degrade. We evaluate on four benchmarks against two baselines, a GCN-based detector and SubgraphAnomaly, and report area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP) over five seeds. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, GraphGuard outperforms SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger baseline, by 0.059 to 0.083 AUC and 0.072 to 0.087 AP. Cora-OOD shows a tie: the two methods differ by 0.003 AUC and 0.004 AP, within seed variation (standard deviation at most 0.006).
