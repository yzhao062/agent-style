Table 3 shows that MedNeXt-Lite achieves higher Dice scores than both baselines for left-ventricle and lung-lesion segmentation. For cardiac MRI, it reaches 89.4%, compared with 85.6% for nnU-Net and 86.2% for SwinUNETR, gaining 3.8 percentage points over nnU-Net (95% CI: 2.4 to 5.2; p = 0.002). For lung CT, it reaches 87.2%, compared with 84.3% and 83.9%, respectively, gaining 2.9 percentage points over nnU-Net (95% CI: 1.1 to 4.7; p = 0.018). For glioma-core segmentation, scores are similar: 88.1% for MedNeXt-Lite, 87.7% for nnU-Net, and 87.9% for SwinUNETR. The difference from nnU-Net remains uncertain (0.4 percentage points; 95% CI: -0.8 to 1.6; p = 0.48). MedNeXt-Lite uses 4.1M parameters versus 31M for nnU-Net, approximately 87% fewer, supporting task-dependent accuracy gains with a smaller model.
