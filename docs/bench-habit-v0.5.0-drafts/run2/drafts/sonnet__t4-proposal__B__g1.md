### Preliminary Results

We ran a pilot of FedVerify on a 100-node heterogeneous edge testbed where 20% of the clients were Byzantine: they submitted poisoned model updates to degrade the global model. Client data was non-IID by construction: a Dirichlet partition at alpha = 0.2 (Hsu et al. 2019) leaves each client holding mostly a few classes. [Dataset and model: insert before submission.] We compared FedVerify with FedAvg without any defense (McMahan et al. 2017) and with two Byzantine-robust aggregators, Krum (Blanchard et al. 2017) and FL-Trust (Cao et al. 2021). Table 4 reports rounds and wall-clock time to reach 80% accuracy, final accuracy, and communication per round.

Without a defense, FedAvg never reaches 80% accuracy and ends at 34.2%, so poisoned updates and skewed client data together defeat undefended training. Among the defenses, FedVerify converges fastest. It reaches 80% in 115 rounds, against 210 for FL-Trust and 340 for Krum, which is 45% and 66% fewer rounds. In wall-clock time, FedVerify needs 5.6 hours, against 11.2 hours for FL-Trust (half) and 14.8 hours for Krum (62% less). FedVerify also reaches the highest final accuracy, 85.4%, which is 2.3 points above FL-Trust (83.1%) and 4.4 points above Krum (81.0%). Without intervals on these point estimates, we read the 2.3-point margin over FL-Trust, the smallest gap in the table, as suggestive.

The wall-clock advantage of FedVerify comes from needing fewer rounds. Dividing wall-clock time by rounds gives 2.9 minutes per round for FedVerify, 12% above Krum (2.6 minutes) and 9% below FL-Trust (3.2 minutes). Communication per round splits the same way: FedVerify sends 14.1 MB, which is 1.7 MB (14%) more than FedAvg and Krum at 12.4 MB each, and 4.1 MB (23%) less than FL-Trust at 18.2 MB. Multiplying per-round communication by rounds to 80% gives about 1.6 GB for FedVerify, against 3.8 GB for FL-Trust and 4.2 GB for Krum.
