Across the three larger datasets in Table 6, augmentation changes accuracy by at most 0.5 points in either direction. On Adult and Credit-default, TabMix and SMOTE differ by only 0.1 points, within one standard deviation, and both sit 0.1 to 0.5 points above the no-augmentation baseline. Covertype reverses this pattern: both methods lower accuracy, TabMix by 0.2 points and SMOTE by 0.5. TabMix's one large gain comes on Churn-small (2,000 rows), where it reaches 81.7%, 5.3 points above no augmentation and 3.7 points above SMOTE. These gaps are several times the run-to-run standard deviation of about 1 point. Because Churn-small is the only small dataset in our comparison, these results suggest, but do not establish, that TabMix helps most when training data are scarce.
