Graph anomaly detection requires methods that learn from unlabeled data while handling dense community structure. Existing approaches either depend on manual anomaly labels or lose detection accuracy on graphs with densely connected communities. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised method that incorporates community structure into contrastive pretraining. GraphGuard trains representations with an objective that contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries, using these comparisons to learn from graph structure without manual labels. This design targets the difficulty of detecting anomalies in graphs with dense communities. We evaluate GraphGuard against GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on four datasets, reporting mean performance over five random seeds in Table 1. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, GraphGuard exceeds the stronger baseline, SubgraphAnomaly, by 5.9 to 8.3 percentage points in area under the receiver operating characteristic curve and 7.2 to 8.7 points in average precision. On Cora-OOD, the methods perform similarly, with differences of just 0.3 and 0.4 points, respectively. Standard deviations across seeds are at most 0.006 for every reported result.
