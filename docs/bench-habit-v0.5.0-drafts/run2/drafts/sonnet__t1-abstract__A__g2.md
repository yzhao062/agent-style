Graph anomaly detection identifies nodes whose connections or attributes deviate from the rest of a graph, such as fraudulent accounts in a transaction network. Existing methods either require manual anomaly labels or degrade on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that needs no labels. GraphGuard pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective that compares subgraphs across community boundaries. We evaluate GraphGuard on four datasets (Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD) against a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger baseline on every dataset. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, GraphGuard raises area under the ROC curve (AUC) over SubgraphAnomaly by 0.083, 0.081, and 0.059 (means over five seeds). Average precision (AP) rises by 0.087, 0.080, and 0.072 on the same datasets. The largest gain appears on Reddit-B, where AUC rises from 0.781 to 0.864. On Cora-OOD, the two methods tie: AUC is 0.891 for GraphGuard and 0.888 for SubgraphAnomaly, a gap within the 0.006 bound on seed standard deviation.
