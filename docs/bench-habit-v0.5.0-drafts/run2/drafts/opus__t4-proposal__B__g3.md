### Preliminary Results

Our pilot study measured whether FedVerify trains an accurate model when one in five edge clients is adversarial, and what that robustness costs in communication. Compared with the fastest baseline, FL-Trust, FedVerify reached 80% test accuracy in half the wall-clock time and sent less than half as much total data (Table 4).

We ran the pilot on a 100-node heterogeneous edge testbed. Twenty of the 100 clients were Byzantine: they sent poisoned model updates intended to corrupt the shared model. We split the training data non-IID, so each client holds a different mix of classes. Each client's class proportions come from a Dirichlet distribution with concentration α = 0.2 (Hsu et al. 2019). At this setting, most of a client's local data comes from a few classes. The pilot covers one operating point: 20% Byzantine clients under strong label skew.

We compared FedVerify with three aggregation rules, which are the methods a server uses to combine client updates. FedAvg (McMahan et al. 2017) averages client updates and has no defense. Krum (Blanchard et al. 2017) selects the single client update with the smallest summed distance to its nearest neighbors. FL-Trust (Cao et al. 2021) weights each client update by its cosine similarity to an update the server computes on a small clean dataset.

Undefended training fails under this attack: FedAvg never reaches 80% accuracy and ends at 34.2%. All three defenses pass 80%, and FedVerify gets there first. It needs 115 rounds, 45% fewer than FL-Trust (210) and 66% fewer than Krum (340). FedVerify reaches 80% in 5.6 hours of wall-clock time, which is 50% of FL-Trust's 11.2 hours and 38% of Krum's 14.8 hours. The speedup comes mainly from needing fewer rounds. Per round (wall-clock time divided by rounds), FedVerify averages 2.9 minutes, 12% longer than Krum (2.6 minutes) and 9% shorter than FL-Trust (3.2 minutes). FedVerify also has the highest final accuracy, 85.4%, which is 2.3 points above FL-Trust and 4.4 points above Krum.

FedVerify sends more data per round than Krum but less in total. At 14.1 MB per round, it transmits 1.7 MB (14%) more than FedAvg or Krum and 4.1 MB (23%) less than FL-Trust. Multiplying per-round volume by rounds to 80% accuracy gives cumulative traffic of 1.6 GB for FedVerify, 3.8 GB for FL-Trust, and 4.2 GB for Krum. On metered or battery-powered edge links, this cumulative figure sets the communication cost of training.
