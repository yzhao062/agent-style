In Table 7, TrafficCast's advantage over the baselines shrinks and then reverses as sensor networks get smaller. On the two largest networks, TrafficCast lowers 60-minute MAE relative to GraphWaveNet, the strongest baseline, by 0.41 km/h on Metro-A (11.6%) and 0.35 km/h on Metro-B (10.6%). Both margins are about eight times the seed standard deviation. Coastal (640 sensors) yields a tie, since the 0.02 km/h gap lies within the seed-to-seed spread. Rural-Net (180 sensors) reverses the ranking and makes TrafficCast the least accurate learned model. Its MAE there exceeds GraphWaveNet's by 0.19 km/h, about twice the seed standard deviation. The accuracy gains also cost latency: TrafficCast takes 41 ms per batch, 12 ms more than GraphWaveNet. These results favor TrafficCast on large metropolitan networks such as Metro-A and Metro-B. On networks of a few hundred sensors, GraphWaveNet matches or beats it at lower latency.
