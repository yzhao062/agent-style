Graph anomaly detection identifies entities whose attributes or connections deviate from the rest of a graph, such as illicit transactions in a Bitcoin payment network. Supervised detectors require manual anomaly labels, which are costly to collect, and existing unsupervised detectors lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective pairing subgraphs across community boundaries. On four benchmarks, we compare GraphGuard with a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly. We report mean area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP) over five seeds; standard deviations are at most 0.006. GraphGuard outperforms GCN on all four benchmarks by 0.079 to 0.122 AUC. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, it also beats the stronger baseline, SubgraphAnomaly, by 0.059 to 0.083 AUC and 0.072 to 0.087 AP. Cora-OOD is the exception: GraphGuard leads SubgraphAnomaly by only 0.003 AUC (0.891 vs. 0.888) and 0.004 AP, a margin comparable to the seed-to-seed standard deviation.
