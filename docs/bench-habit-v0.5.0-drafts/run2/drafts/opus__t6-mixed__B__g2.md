Table 6 reports mean accuracy over 10 runs. Across the three larger datasets (Adult, Credit-default, Covertype), all three methods lie within 0.5 points of each other. Adult shows the smallest spread: the methods differ by at most 0.2 points, within one standard deviation. Both augmentations help slightly on Credit-default, raising accuracy by 0.4 (TabMix) and 0.5 (SMOTE) points, about one standard deviation. Covertype shows the reverse: both augmentations lower accuracy, TabMix by 0.2 points and SMOTE by 0.5 points. Churn-small, with only 2,000 rows, is the exception. Here TabMix reaches 81.7%, which is 5.3 points above no augmentation and 3.7 points above SMOTE. Both gaps are more than three times the largest standard deviation. TabMix clearly beats both baselines only in this small-data setting, and our study includes just one dataset of that kind.
