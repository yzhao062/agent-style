### Preliminary Results

Our pilot tests whether FedVerify keeps a federated model accurate when one in five clients sends poisoned updates, and what that protection costs in time and bandwidth. In federated learning, edge devices (clients) train a shared model on local data and send only model updates to a server. We call a client Byzantine when it sends arbitrary or adversarial updates. In the pilot, Byzantine clients poison their updates to degrade the global model.

The pilot ran on a testbed of 100 heterogeneous edge devices, 20 of them Byzantine. Real edge data is non-IID: each device's data follows a different distribution. To reproduce this, we split the training data across clients with a Dirichlet distribution at concentration α = 0.2 (Hsu et al., 2019). Lower α gives each client a more skewed class mix. At 0.2, a few classes dominate most clients' data.

Table 4 compares FedVerify with three baselines. FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) averages all client updates and has no defense. Krum (Blanchard et al., 2017) keeps the single update with the smallest total distance to its nearest neighbors. FL-Trust (Cao et al., 2021) weights each update by its cosine similarity to an update the server computes on a small clean dataset. For each method, we report four measures: rounds and wall-clock hours to reach 80% test accuracy, final accuracy, and data transmitted per round.

The attack works: FedAvg never reaches 80% accuracy and finishes at 34.2%. All three defenses restore training, and FedVerify restores it fastest. FedVerify reaches 80% in 115 rounds, 45% fewer than FL-Trust (210) and 66% fewer than Krum (340). Its wall-clock time to 80% is 5.6 hours, half of FL-Trust's 11.2 hours and 38% of Krum's 14.8 hours. Final accuracy follows the same order, with FedVerify ending at 85.4%, 2.3 points above FL-Trust and 4.4 points above Krum.

FedVerify transmits 14.1 MB per round, 1.7 MB (14%) more than FedAvg and Krum and 4.1 MB (23%) less than FL-Trust. Because it needs fewer rounds, FedVerify still sends the least total data before reaching 80% accuracy. Multiplying rounds by per-round communication gives 1.6 GB for FedVerify, 3.8 GB for FL-Trust, and 4.2 GB for Krum. Dividing wall-clock time by rounds shows the same pattern for time: a FedVerify round takes 2.9 minutes, against 2.6 for Krum and 3.2 for FL-Trust. FedVerify's wall-clock advantage therefore comes entirely from its lower round count.
