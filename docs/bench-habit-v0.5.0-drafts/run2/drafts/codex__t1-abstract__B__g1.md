Graph anomaly detection seeks to identify unusual graph patterns, but manual labeling and dense community structure limit existing methods. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised graph anomaly detection method that pretrains with a contrastive objective comparing subgraphs across community boundaries. This objective makes community boundaries an explicit part of pretraining and requires no manual anomaly labels. We evaluate GraphGuard against GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD using area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and average precision (AP). GraphGuard achieves the highest mean AUC and AP on all four datasets (Table 1). On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, it improves AUC by 5.9 to 8.3 percentage points and AP by 7.2 to 8.7 points over SubgraphAnomaly. The methods are nearly tied on Cora-OOD, where GraphGuard improves AUC and AP by 0.3 and 0.4 points, respectively. Results average five seeds, with standard deviations at most 0.006 for every method and metric. These findings support community-boundary contrastive pretraining for unsupervised graph anomaly detection, with the clearest gains on three of the four benchmarks.
