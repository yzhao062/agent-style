Graph anomaly detection finds nodes whose behavior deviates from the rest of a network, such as fraudulent accounts in a transaction graph. Existing methods either require manual anomaly labels, which are costly to collect, or degrade on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised method that pretrains with a contrastive objective over subgraphs drawn from across community boundaries. The objective needs no labels, and its cross-boundary contrast targets the dense-community graphs where existing methods degrade. Experiments on four datasets (Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD) compare GraphGuard with GCN, a graph convolutional network, and SubgraphAnomaly. We report mean area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP) over five seeds, with standard deviations of at most 0.006. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, GraphGuard outperforms the strongest baseline, SubgraphAnomaly, by 0.083, 0.081, and 0.059 AUC and by 0.087, 0.080, and 0.072 AP. The gap shrinks to 0.003 AUC (0.891 versus 0.888) and 0.004 AP on Cora-OOD, below the 0.006 upper bound on seed standard deviation.
