Across the four networks in Table 7, TrafficCast's advantage shrinks as the sensor count falls. On Metro-A and Metro-B, the two largest networks, TrafficCast lowers 60-minute MAE by 0.41 and 0.35 km/h (about 11%) relative to GraphWaveNet, the strongest baseline. Both gaps are roughly eight times the standard deviation across seeds. On Coastal (640 sensors), the two models tie: they differ by 0.02 km/h, well within one standard deviation. Rural-Net (180 sensors) reverses the ranking. There, TrafficCast trails GraphWaveNet by 0.19 km/h, about twice the standard deviation across seeds, and also falls behind DCRNN. With only four networks, sensor count may stand in for other differences among them, so we treat the size trend as suggestive. TrafficCast is also slower at inference, taking 41 ms per batch against 29 ms for GraphWaveNet, a 41% increase. On the metro networks, the extra 12 ms buys an 11% error reduction; on Coastal and Rural-Net, GraphWaveNet matches or beats TrafficCast at lower cost.
