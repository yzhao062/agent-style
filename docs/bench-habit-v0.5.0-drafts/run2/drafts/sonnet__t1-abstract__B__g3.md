Graph anomaly detection flags nodes whose behavior deviates from the rest of a network, for example fraudulent accounts, bots, or illicit transactions. Existing detectors either need manual anomaly labels, which are costly to collect, or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that requires no labels. GraphGuard pretrains an encoder with a contrastive objective (a loss that pulls similar examples together and pushes dissimilar ones apart) that contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries. We compare it with GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on four datasets, reporting AUC (area under the ROC curve) and average precision (AP) as means over 5 seeds. GraphGuard improves AUC over the stronger baseline, SubgraphAnomaly, by 5.9 to 8.3 points and AP by 7.2 to 8.7 points on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic. On the fourth dataset, Cora-OOD, GraphGuard ties SubgraphAnomaly: it leads by 0.003 AUC and 0.004 AP, comparable to the seed-to-seed standard deviation of up to 0.006.
