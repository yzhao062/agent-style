Graph anomaly detection faces two recurring limitations: dependence on manual labels and reduced accuracy on graphs with densely connected communities. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised method that pretrains graph representations with a contrastive objective comparing subgraphs across community boundaries. These comparisons incorporate community structure into pretraining without requiring anomaly labels. We evaluate GraphGuard against a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD, reporting means over five seeds. Across all four datasets, GraphGuard achieves higher area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and average precision (AP) than both baselines. Relative to SubgraphAnomaly, AUC increases by 0.083 on Reddit-B, 0.081 on Twitch-EN, and 0.059 on Elliptic. Corresponding AP gains are 0.087, 0.080, and 0.072, respectively. On Cora-OOD, GraphGuard reaches 0.891 AUC and 0.835 AP, with smaller gains of 0.003 and 0.004 over SubgraphAnomaly. Standard deviations across seeds are at most 0.006 for every reported score, indicating low variability across the five runs.
