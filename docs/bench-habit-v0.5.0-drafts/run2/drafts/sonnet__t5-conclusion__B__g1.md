## Conclusion

AdaptRL transfers grasping policies from simulation to a real robot by learning residual dynamics online. Residual dynamics capture the difference between the simulator's predictions and the robot's observed behavior. In 100 real-robot trials per object category (Table 5), AdaptRL achieved the highest grasp success rate in all five categories, from 94% on rigid objects to 68% on transparent objects. AdaptRL outperformed domain randomization by 23 points on rigid objects and by 36 to 44 points on the other four categories. Its margin over DAgger was 11 points on rigid objects and 18 to 24 points on the other four.

All three methods rank the categories identically by success rate: rigid, deformable, jointed, reflective, transparent. The margin over DAgger widens from 11 points on rigid objects to 24 points on reflective objects, then narrows slightly to 22 points on transparent objects. This pattern fits the hypothesis that online residual learning helps most where simulation deviates most from reality. The trials do not measure that deviation directly. Transparent objects remain the weakest category: AdaptRL fails on 32 of 100 transparent grasps, against 21 of 100 reflective grasps.
