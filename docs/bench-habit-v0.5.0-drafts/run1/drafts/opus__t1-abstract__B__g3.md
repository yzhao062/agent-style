Graph anomaly detection flags nodes, edges, or subgraphs whose attributes or connections deviate from normal patterns, such as illicit transactions in a Bitcoin transaction graph. Current detectors either require manually labeled anomalies or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that needs no anomaly labels. GraphGuard pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective, a loss that trains the encoder by comparing pairs of examples. Here, each pair consists of two subgraphs drawn from opposite sides of a community boundary. We evaluate GraphGuard on four benchmarks against a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly, averaging over five random seeds (standard deviation at most 0.006). GraphGuard exceeds GCN on all four benchmarks by 0.079 to 0.122 AUC. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, it also outperforms SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger baseline, by 0.059 to 0.083 AUC and 0.072 to 0.087 average precision. Cora-OOD is the exception: there, the two methods score within seed variation of each other (AUC 0.891 vs. 0.888; average precision 0.835 vs. 0.831).
