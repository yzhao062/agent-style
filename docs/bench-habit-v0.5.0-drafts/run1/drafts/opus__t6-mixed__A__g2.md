Table 6 reports accuracy (mean ± standard deviation over 10 runs) for no augmentation, SMOTE, and TabMix on four datasets. On Adult, Credit-default, and Covertype, the three settings differ by at most 0.5 points. SMOTE scores highest on Adult (85.3%) and Credit-default (82.4%), with TabMix 0.1 points behind on each. That gap is smaller than any standard deviation on those two datasets (0.2 to 0.5 points). On Covertype, both augmentation methods lower accuracy. Training without augmentation reaches 92.6 ± 0.1%, compared with 92.4 ± 0.2% for TabMix and 92.1 ± 0.2% for SMOTE. The clearest difference appears on Churn-small, which has only 2,000 rows. There, TabMix reaches 81.7 ± 0.9%, a gain of 5.3 points over no augmentation (76.4 ± 1.1%) and 3.7 points over SMOTE (78.0 ± 1.0%). Both gaps are more than three times the largest standard deviation on that dataset. Churn-small is the only small dataset in our evaluation, so the evidence that TabMix helps most when training data is scarce rests on this single dataset.
