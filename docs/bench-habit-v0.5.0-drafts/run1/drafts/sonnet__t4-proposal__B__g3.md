### Preliminary Results

We ran a pilot of FedVerify on a 100-node heterogeneous edge testbed. Of the participating clients, 20% were Byzantine, meaning they sent poisoned model updates to corrupt the global model. Client data were non-IID, meaning not independent and identically distributed. We drew each client's class proportions from a Dirichlet distribution with α = 0.2, which skews each client's data toward a few classes.

The pilot compared FedVerify with three baselines. FedAvg (McMahan et al. 2017) averages client updates with no defense. Krum (Blanchard et al. 2017) keeps only the client update closest to its nearest neighbors in parameter space. FL-Trust (Cao et al. 2021) weights each update by its similarity to one the server computes on a small clean dataset. Table 4 reports four measurements for each method: rounds to 80% accuracy, wall-clock time to 80%, final accuracy, and communication per round. A round is one cycle of model distribution, local training, and update aggregation.

In this pilot configuration, the 20% poisoning defeats undefended aggregation: FedAvg ends at 34.2% accuracy and never reaches 80%. All three defenses reach 80%, and FedVerify is the fastest of them, at 115 rounds against 210 for FL-Trust and 340 for Krum. That is 45% fewer rounds than FL-Trust and 66% fewer than Krum. Wall-clock time shows the same ordering: FedVerify takes 5.6 hours to reach 80%, half of FL-Trust's 11.2 hours and 62% below Krum's 14.8 hours. Its final accuracy of 85.4% also leads the four methods, 2.3 points above FL-Trust and 4.4 points above Krum.

The wall-clock gain comes from needing fewer rounds. Dividing wall-clock time by rounds gives about 175 seconds per round for FedVerify, 12% slower than Krum (157 seconds) and 9% faster than FL-Trust (192 seconds). Per-round communication also falls between the baselines: 14.1 MB for FedVerify, 14% above FedAvg and Krum (12.4 MB) and 23% below FL-Trust (18.2 MB). Fewer rounds more than offset the extra bytes. Reaching 80% accuracy takes 1.6 GB of cumulative communication with FedVerify (115 rounds times 14.1 MB), 3.8 GB with FL-Trust, and 4.2 GB with Krum.
