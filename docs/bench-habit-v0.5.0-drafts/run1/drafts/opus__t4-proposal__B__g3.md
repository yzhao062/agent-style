### Preliminary Results

Our pilot measured how quickly FedVerify trains an accurate model when one-fifth of its clients are adversarial, and how much communication that training costs. We ran it on a 100-node heterogeneous edge testbed. In each training round, the server sends the current global model to the clients, and each client trains it on local data. The server then aggregates the returned updates into a new global model. Twenty of the 100 clients were Byzantine, meaning they could send arbitrary updates; in the pilot, they sent poisoned updates designed to degrade the global model. Client data were non-IID (not independent and identically distributed). We drew each client's label proportions from a Dirichlet distribution with concentration α = 0.2. Smaller α concentrates each client's data in fewer classes, so 0.2 produces strongly skewed clients.

We compared FedVerify with three baselines. FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) averages client updates with no defense. Krum (Blanchard et al., 2017) keeps the one update whose summed distance to its nearest neighbors is smallest. FL-Trust (Cao et al., 2021) weights each client update by its cosine similarity to an update the server computes on a small clean dataset. Table 4 lists each method's rounds and wall-clock hours to reach 80% accuracy, its final accuracy, and its communication per round.

The attack is strong enough to break undefended training: FedAvg never reaches 80% accuracy and finishes at 34.2%. Among the defenses, FedVerify reaches 80% accuracy 2.0× faster in wall-clock time than FL-Trust and 2.6× faster than Krum (Table 4). The speedup comes from convergence in fewer rounds: FedVerify needs 45% fewer rounds than FL-Trust and 66% fewer than Krum. Its time per round, computed as wall-clock time divided by rounds, is about 2.9 minutes. That cost sits 9% below FL-Trust (3.2 minutes) and 12% above Krum (2.6 minutes), so FedVerify beats Krum on wall-clock time despite a slower round.

FedVerify also finishes with the highest accuracy, 85.4%, which is 2.3 points above FL-Trust and 4.4 points above Krum. Its per-round communication is 14% above FedAvg and Krum and 23% below FL-Trust. The per-round figure hides FedVerify's advantage, because FedVerify runs far fewer rounds. Cumulative communication to reach 80% accuracy (rounds × per-round communication) is about 1.6 GB for FedVerify, against 3.8 GB for FL-Trust and 4.2 GB for Krum. On bandwidth-limited edge links, total traffic matters more than per-round size, and FedVerify's total is 58% below FL-Trust's and 62% below Krum's.
