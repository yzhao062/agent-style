Table 3 shows that MedNeXt-Lite achieved the highest mean Dice across all three tasks, with statistically significant gains over nnU-Net for cardiac and lung segmentation. For left-ventricle segmentation, Dice increased from 85.6% to 89.4%, a 3.8-point gain (95% bootstrap confidence interval [CI], 2.4 to 5.2; p = 0.002). Lung-lesion segmentation improved from 84.3% to 87.2%, a 2.9-point gain (95% CI, 1.1 to 4.7; p = 0.018). For glioma-core segmentation, the difference was small and uncertain: 88.1% versus 87.7%, a 0.4-point gain (95% CI, -0.8 to 1.6; p = 0.48). This comparison establishes neither superiority nor equivalence. Relative to SwinUNETR, MedNeXt-Lite achieved mean Dice gains of 3.2, 3.3, and 0.2 points for cardiac, lung, and brain segmentation, respectively; statistical significance remains unassessed. MedNeXt-Lite used 4.1 million parameters versus nnU-Net’s 31 million, a reduction of approximately 87%, while improving Dice on two tasks. The smaller parameter count supports a reduced model footprint; inference speed and clinical benefit require separate evaluation.
