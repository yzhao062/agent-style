Graph anomaly detection requires distinguishing unusual graph structure from patterns associated with densely connected communities. Existing methods either require manual labels or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised graph anomaly detection method that incorporates community boundaries into contrastive pretraining. GraphGuard learns subgraph representations through an objective that contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries. This design uses community structure to guide representation learning without requiring manually assigned anomaly labels. We evaluate GraphGuard against GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on four datasets using area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and average precision (AP). GraphGuard achieves the highest mean AUC and AP on every dataset (Table 1). On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, it improves AUC over SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger baseline, by 5.9 to 8.3 percentage points. AP gains on these datasets range from 7.2 to 8.7 percentage points. Cora-OOD shows near parity, with improvements below half a percentage point in both metrics. Across five random seeds, standard deviations are at most 0.006 for every reported score.
