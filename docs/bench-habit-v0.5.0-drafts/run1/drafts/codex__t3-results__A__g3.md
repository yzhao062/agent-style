Table 3 shows that MedNeXt-Lite improves segmentation Dice over nnU-Net for the cardiac and lung tasks, while evidence of a brain-task improvement remains inconclusive. For left-ventricle segmentation on cardiac MRI, MedNeXt-Lite achieves 89.4% Dice versus 85.6% for nnU-Net, a gain of 3.8 percentage points (95% bootstrap CI [2.4, 5.2]; p = 0.002). On lung CT lesions, Dice increases from 84.3% to 87.2%, a gain of 2.9 percentage points (95% bootstrap CI [1.1, 4.7]; p = 0.018). For glioma-core segmentation on brain MRI, MedNeXt-Lite achieves 88.1% Dice versus 87.7% for nnU-Net. The estimated difference is 0.4 percentage points (95% bootstrap CI [-0.8, 1.6]; p = 0.48), leaving superiority and equivalence unestablished. MedNeXt-Lite also exceeds SwinUNETR’s mean Dice by 3.2, 3.3, and 0.2 percentage points for the cardiac, lung, and brain tasks, respectively. These comparisons remain descriptive because statistical tests against SwinUNETR are unavailable. The cardiac and lung gains accompany a reduction from 31 million to 4.1 million parameters, approximately 87% fewer than nnU-Net.
