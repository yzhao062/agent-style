Graph anomaly detection faces two recurring limitations: dependence on manual labels and reduced accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised method that pretrains graph representations with a contrastive objective comparing subgraphs across community boundaries. Communities are groups of densely connected nodes. By using their boundaries to organize subgraph comparisons, GraphGuard incorporates community structure into pretraining without requiring anomaly labels. We evaluate GraphGuard against GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD, averaging results over five seeds. GraphGuard achieves higher mean area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and average precision (AP) than both baselines on all four datasets. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, it improves AUC over SubgraphAnomaly by 0.083, 0.081, and 0.059, respectively. Corresponding AP gains are 0.087, 0.080, and 0.072. Improvements on Cora-OOD are modest: AUC rises from 0.888 to 0.891, while AP rises from 0.831 to 0.835. Standard deviations across seeds are at most 0.006 for every reported result. These evaluations support GraphGuard’s effectiveness across the tested datasets, with gains that vary substantially by dataset.
