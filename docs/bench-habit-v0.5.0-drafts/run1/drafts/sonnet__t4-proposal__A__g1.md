### Preliminary Results

We piloted FedVerify on a 100-node heterogeneous edge testbed to measure its resistance to model poisoning on diverse edge hardware. Twenty of the 100 clients (20%) acted as Byzantine clients, which send corrupted model updates to degrade the shared model. Client data were non-IID (not independent and identically distributed), simulated with a Dirichlet label partition at concentration alpha = 0.2 (Hsu et al. 2019). A small alpha concentrates each client's data on a few classes. The baselines are FedAvg, which averages all client updates with no defense (McMahan et al. 2017), and two Byzantine-robust aggregation rules, Krum (Blanchard et al. 2017) and FL-Trust (Cao et al. 2021). Table 4 reports four measures per method: rounds to 80% accuracy, wall-clock time to 80% accuracy, final accuracy, and communication per round.

Undefended FedAvg never reached 80% accuracy and ended at 34.2%. FedVerify reached 80% accuracy in 115 rounds and 5.6 hours. FL-Trust needed 210 rounds and 11.2 hours, and Krum needed 340 rounds and 14.8 hours. FedVerify therefore used 45% fewer rounds and 50% less wall-clock time than FL-Trust, the faster of the two robust baselines. Against Krum, it used 66% fewer rounds and 62% less wall-clock time. Averaged over the rounds to 80%, per-round time is similar across the three robust methods (2.6 minutes for Krum, 2.9 for FedVerify, 3.2 for FL-Trust), so the speedup comes from FedVerify needing fewer rounds. FedVerify also ended with the highest accuracy, 85.4%, which is 2.3 points above FL-Trust (83.1%) and 4.4 points above Krum (81.0%).

FedVerify sends 14.1 MB per round. That is 1.7 MB (14%) above the 12.4 MB of FedAvg and Krum, and 4.1 MB (23%) below the 18.2 MB of FL-Trust. Because FedVerify needs fewer rounds, its cumulative volume to reach 80% accuracy (the per-round figure times the rounds) is about 1.6 GB, against 3.8 GB for FL-Trust and 4.2 GB for Krum.

Table 4 gives one value per metric and no confidence intervals, so the pilot indicates feasibility but leaves run-to-run variation unquantified. The pilot also covers a single Byzantine fraction (20%), a single heterogeneity level (alpha = 0.2), and poisoning attacks only. Other Byzantine fractions, other heterogeneity levels, and other attack strategies remain untested.
