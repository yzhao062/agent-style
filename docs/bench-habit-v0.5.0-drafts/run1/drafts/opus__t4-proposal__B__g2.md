### Preliminary Results

We piloted FedVerify on a 100-node edge testbed in which 20% of clients send poisoned model updates. FedVerify reaches 80% accuracy in half the wall-clock time of the strongest baseline, FL-Trust, and with 58% less total traffic (Table 4). The paragraphs below describe the pilot configuration and baselines. They then report attack strength, convergence, and communication cost.

**Pilot Setup.** We ran the pilot on a testbed of 100 heterogeneous edge devices, each acting as one federated learning client. Twenty clients (20%) were Byzantine. A Byzantine participant may behave arbitrarily (Lamport et al., 1982); in this pilot, these clients sent poisoned model updates intended to degrade the global model. Client data was non-IID (not independent and identically distributed), so each client's data followed a different distribution. We drew each client's class proportions from a Dirichlet distribution with concentration α = 0.2, which leaves most clients with data concentrated in a few classes. This skew makes defense harder. At α = 0.2, honest updates also differ from one another, so a defense that discards updates far from the majority can discard honest ones too. All results below come from this single operating point.

**Baselines.** We compared FedVerify with three methods, all run under the same configuration. FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) averages client updates and has no defense. Krum (Blanchard et al., 2017) selects the single client update with the smallest sum of squared distances to its nearest neighbors. FL-Trust (Cao et al., 2021) weights each client update by its cosine similarity to a reference update that the server computes on a small, clean dataset.

**Attack Strength.** The attack breaks undefended training: FedAvg never reaches 80% accuracy and ends at 34.2%. All three defenses reach the 80% target, at different costs in rounds, time, and bandwidth.

**Convergence and Accuracy.** FedVerify reaches 80% accuracy in 115 rounds, 45% fewer than FL-Trust and 66% fewer than Krum. Its wall-clock time to 80% is 5.6 hours, half of FL-Trust's and 38% of Krum's. Dividing wall-clock time by rounds gives similar per-round times for the three defenses: 2.6 minutes for Krum, 2.9 for FedVerify, and 3.2 for FL-Trust. The wall-clock gain therefore comes from needing fewer rounds. Faster convergence also comes with higher final accuracy: FedVerify finishes at 85.4%, 2.3 points above FL-Trust and 4.4 points above Krum.

**Communication Cost.** FedVerify sends 14.1 MB per round, 14% more than FedAvg and Krum and 23% less than FL-Trust. Edge devices on constrained links pay for total traffic, though, and FedVerify's lower round count more than offsets its per-round overhead. Cumulative traffic to 80% accuracy (rounds times per-round traffic) is 1.6 GB for FedVerify, 58% less than FL-Trust's 3.8 GB and 62% less than Krum's 4.2 GB.
