Graph anomaly detection flags nodes whose structure or attributes deviate from the rest of a graph, such as illicit payments in a Bitcoin transaction graph. Current detectors either require manual anomaly labels, which are costly to collect, or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective. The objective contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries, so pretraining targets the dense community structure on which prior unsupervised detectors degrade. We compare GraphGuard with a graph convolutional network and SubgraphAnomaly on four datasets, measuring area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP) over five seeds. Standard deviations across seeds are at most 0.006. GraphGuard outperforms the graph convolutional network on all four datasets by 0.08 to 0.12 AUC. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, it also outperforms SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger baseline, by 0.06 to 0.08 AUC and 0.07 to 0.09 AP. Cora-OOD is the exception: GraphGuard and SubgraphAnomaly differ there by only 0.003 AUC, within seed variation.
