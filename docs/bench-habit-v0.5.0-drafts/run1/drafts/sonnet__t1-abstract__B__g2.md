Graph anomaly detection flags nodes whose structure or attributes deviate from the rest of a graph, as in fraud detection on transaction networks. Existing methods either require manual anomaly labels, which are costly to collect, or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective. The objective contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries and needs no anomaly labels. We evaluate GraphGuard on four benchmarks (Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD) against a GCN baseline and SubgraphAnomaly. Scores are area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP), averaged over five seeds. GraphGuard attains the highest mean AUC and AP on all four datasets. Compared with SubgraphAnomaly, it raises AUC by 0.083 on Reddit-B, 0.081 on Twitch-EN, and 0.059 on Elliptic, and raises AP by 0.087, 0.080, and 0.072. On Cora-OOD, the two methods tie within seed variation (AUC 0.891 versus 0.888, AP 0.835 versus 0.831; standard deviations at most 0.006).
