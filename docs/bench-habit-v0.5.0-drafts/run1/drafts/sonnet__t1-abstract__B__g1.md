Graph anomaly detection flags nodes that deviate from the rest of a graph, such as illicit transactions in a payment network. Existing detectors either require manual anomaly labels or degrade on graphs whose nodes form densely connected communities. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective. The objective contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries. We evaluate GraphGuard against a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD. Reported area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP) are means over five seeds, with standard deviations of at most 0.006. GraphGuard beats GCN on all four datasets by 7.9 to 12.2 AUC points. Against SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger of the two baselines, it improves AUC by 5.9 to 8.3 points and AP by 7.2 to 8.7 points on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic. On Cora-OOD, the two methods nearly tie (AUC 0.891 versus 0.888; AP 0.835 versus 0.831).
