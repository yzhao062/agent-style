Table 6 reports mean accuracy over 10 runs. On the three larger datasets, neither augmentation method changes accuracy by more than 0.5 points. Both methods stay within one standard deviation of the baseline on Adult. On Credit-default, each gains about half a point, which is close to the run-to-run standard deviation of 0.4 to 0.5 points. Covertype is the one dataset where augmentation lowers accuracy: SMOTE costs 0.5 points and TabMix costs 0.2. Churn-small (2,000 rows) is where the two methods differ. There, TabMix raises accuracy from 76.4% to 81.7%, which is 5.3 points above no augmentation and 3.7 points above SMOTE. Both gaps are several times the run-to-run standard deviation of about 1 point. TabMix therefore stays within 0.3 points of SMOTE on the three larger datasets, and its clear advantage appears only on Churn-small, the one small dataset in our evaluation.
