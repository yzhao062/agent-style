Graph anomaly detection finds nodes whose connections or attributes deviate from the rest of a graph, such as fraudulent accounts in a transaction network. Existing detectors either require manual anomaly labels or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure, where nodes cluster into tightly linked groups. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that pretrains with a contrastive objective, which trains a model to separate similar from dissimilar examples. The objective contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries. We evaluate on four benchmarks and average over five seeds. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, GraphGuard improves AUC (area under the ROC curve) by 5.9 to 8.3 points over SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger of two baselines. Average precision (AP) improves by 7.2 to 8.7 points. On Cora-OOD, GraphGuard matches SubgraphAnomaly: AUC differs by 0.003 and AP by 0.004, below the largest seed standard deviation of 0.006. Against a graph convolutional network (GCN) baseline, GraphGuard improves AUC by 7.9 to 12.2 points on all four datasets.
