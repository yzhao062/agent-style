Graph anomaly detection requires distinguishing unusual patterns from the community structure that organizes normal graph behavior. Existing methods either require manual labels or lose accuracy on graphs with dense communities. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised graph anomaly detection method that pretrains graph representations with a contrastive objective. By contrasting subgraphs across community boundaries, this objective makes community structure an explicit part of representation learning. We evaluate GraphGuard against GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on four datasets, reporting area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and average precision (AP), averaged over five seeds. GraphGuard achieves the highest mean AUC and AP on all four datasets (Table 1). Relative to SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger baseline in these experiments, GraphGuard improves AUC by 5.9 to 8.3 percentage points and AP by 7.2 to 8.7 points on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic. On Cora-OOD, the methods perform similarly, with gains of 0.3 AUC points and 0.4 AP points. Standard deviations across seeds remain at most 0.006 for every reported score.
