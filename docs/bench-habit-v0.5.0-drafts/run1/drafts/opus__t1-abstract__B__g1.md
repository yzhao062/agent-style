Graph anomaly detection flags the parts of a graph whose structure or attributes deviate from typical patterns, such as illicit Bitcoin transactions. Existing detectors either require manually labeled anomalies, which are rare and costly to collect, or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that needs no anomaly labels. It pretrains a graph encoder with a contrastive objective that compares subgraphs across community boundaries. We evaluate GraphGuard against GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on four benchmarks covering social platforms (Reddit-B, Twitch-EN), Bitcoin transactions (Elliptic), and citations (Cora-OOD). Each result averages five seeds, with standard deviations of at most 0.006. GraphGuard outperforms GCN on every benchmark by 0.08 to 0.12 in area under the ROC curve (AUC). On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, it improves over SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger baseline, by 0.06 to 0.08 AUC and 0.07 to 0.09 average precision (AP). The two methods perform comparably on Cora-OOD, where the gaps shrink to 0.003 AUC and 0.004 AP.
