Graph anomaly detection finds nodes or subgraphs that deviate from the rest of a graph. Existing methods either need manually labeled anomalies or degrade on graphs with dense community structure. Such graphs contain groups of nodes (communities) that link far more tightly to each other than to the rest of the graph. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector. It pretrains an encoder with a contrastive objective (learning by comparing pairs of examples) that contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries. We evaluate GraphGuard on four datasets: Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD. Baselines are a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly. We report area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP), averaged over five seeds. Against SubgraphAnomaly, GraphGuard raises AUC by 0.083 on Reddit-B, 0.081 on Twitch-EN, and 0.059 on Elliptic, and raises AP by 0.087, 0.080, and 0.072 on the same datasets. On Cora-OOD, GraphGuard matches SubgraphAnomaly (AUC 0.891 versus 0.888; AP 0.835 versus 0.831), with gaps smaller than the maximum seed standard deviation of 0.006. GraphGuard also exceeds the GCN on all four datasets, by 0.079 to 0.122 AUC.
