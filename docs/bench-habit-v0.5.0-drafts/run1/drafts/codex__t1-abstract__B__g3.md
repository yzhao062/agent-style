Detecting graph anomalies without manual labels remains challenging on graphs with dense community structure. Existing methods either require manual labels or lose accuracy on such graphs. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised graph anomaly detection method that uses community boundaries to guide contrastive pretraining. Its objective contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries, learning representations through structural comparisons without manually labeled anomalies. This approach makes community structure an explicit part of pretraining for anomaly detection. We evaluate GraphGuard against GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD using area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and average precision (AP). Averaged over five seeds, GraphGuard improves over SubgraphAnomaly on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic by 5.9 to 8.3 percentage points in AUC and 7.2 to 8.7 points in AP (Table 1). On Cora-OOD, the methods perform comparably, with gains of 0.3 AUC points and 0.4 AP points. Standard deviations across seeds are at most 0.006 for every reported score.
