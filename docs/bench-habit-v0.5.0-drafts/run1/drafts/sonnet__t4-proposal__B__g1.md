### Preliminary Results

We ran a pilot study of FedVerify on a 100-node heterogeneous edge testbed. Twenty of the 100 nodes acted as Byzantine clients, meaning they poisoned training by sending corrupted model updates to the server. Client data was non-IID (not independent and identically distributed): we partitioned labels with a Dirichlet distribution at α = 0.2, so each client's labels concentrate in a few classes. We compared FedVerify against FedAvg with no defense (McMahan et al. 2017), Krum (Blanchard et al. 2017), and FL-Trust (Cao et al. 2021). Table 4 reports rounds and wall-clock time to reach 80% accuracy, final accuracy, and communication per round.

In this setting, FedAvg never reaches 80% accuracy and ends at 34.2%, 51.2 points below FedVerify. Among the defended methods, FedVerify converges fastest: it reaches 80% in 115 rounds, against 210 for FL-Trust and 340 for Krum, reductions of 45% and 66%. Wall-clock time to 80% falls from 11.2 h (FL-Trust) and 14.8 h (Krum) to 5.6 h, reductions of 50% and 62%. The speedup comes from fewer rounds; FedVerify averages 2.9 minutes per round, between Krum (2.6) and FL-Trust (3.2). FedVerify also ends highest, at 85.4% final accuracy, 2.3 points above FL-Trust (83.1%) and 4.4 points above Krum (81.0%).

FedVerify sends 14.1 MB per round. That is 1.7 MB (14%) more than FedAvg and Krum at 12.4 MB, and 4.1 MB (23%) less than FL-Trust at 18.2 MB. Multiplying rounds to 80% accuracy by per-round communication gives total traffic of about 1.6 GB for FedVerify, 3.8 GB for FL-Trust, and 4.2 GB for Krum. FedVerify therefore moves 58% fewer bytes than FL-Trust and 62% fewer than Krum before reaching the accuracy target.
