Table 6 reports accuracy (mean ± std over 10 runs) for TabMix, SMOTE, and a no-augmentation baseline on four datasets. On the three larger datasets (Adult, Credit-default, and Covertype), all methods fall within 0.5 points of one another. Augmentation adds at most 0.5 points on Adult and Credit-default, a gap comparable to the run-to-run standard deviation of 0.2 to 0.5 points. SMOTE edges TabMix by 0.1 points on both. On Covertype, both augmentation methods lower accuracy slightly, from 92.6% without augmentation to 92.4% with TabMix and 92.1% with SMOTE. Churn-small, with only 2,000 rows, separates the methods clearly. TabMix reaches 81.7 ± 0.9 there, 5.3 points above no augmentation (76.4 ± 1.1) and 3.7 points above SMOTE (78.0 ± 1.0). Both gaps exceed three times the per-method standard deviation. These results suggest that TabMix helps most when training data are scarce and adds little on larger datasets. Because Churn-small is our only low-data benchmark, this trend needs confirmation on additional small datasets.
