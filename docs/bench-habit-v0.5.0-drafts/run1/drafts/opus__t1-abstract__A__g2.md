Graph anomaly detection flags nodes whose attributes or connections deviate from the rest of a graph, such as fraudulent accounts in a transaction network. Existing detectors either require manually labeled anomalies or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that removes the need for anomaly labels. To handle dense communities, GraphGuard pretrains a graph encoder by contrasting subgraphs across community boundaries. We compare GraphGuard with a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly on four benchmarks, reporting area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP) over five seeds. Standard deviations across seeds are at most 0.006. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, GraphGuard outperforms SubgraphAnomaly, the stronger baseline, by 0.059 to 0.083 AUC and 0.072 to 0.087 AP. Reddit-B shows the largest AUC gain, from 0.781 to 0.864. On Cora-OOD, the two methods perform comparably (AUC 0.891 versus 0.888; AP 0.835 versus 0.831), with gaps comparable to the seed standard deviation.
