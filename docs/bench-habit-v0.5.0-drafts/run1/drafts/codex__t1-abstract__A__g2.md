Existing graph anomaly detection methods require manual labels or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised method that uses community boundaries to guide contrastive pretraining. Its objective contrasts subgraphs across community boundaries, providing a training signal without manually labeled anomalies. We evaluate GraphGuard against GCN and SubgraphAnomaly on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD, averaging anomaly detection results over five random seeds. Evaluation uses area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and average precision (AP). In Table 1, GraphGuard achieves higher mean AUC and AP than both baselines on all four datasets. Relative to SubgraphAnomaly, it improves AUC by 0.083, 0.081, and 0.059 on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, respectively. Corresponding AP gains are 0.087, 0.080, and 0.072. On Cora-OOD, gains are smaller: AUC increases from 0.888 to 0.891, and AP increases from 0.831 to 0.835. Standard deviations across seeds are at most 0.006 for every reported score.
