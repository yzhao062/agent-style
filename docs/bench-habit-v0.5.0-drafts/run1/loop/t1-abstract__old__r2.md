Graph anomaly detection finds nodes or subgraphs that deviate from the rest of a graph. Existing methods either require manual anomaly labels or lose accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that pretrains an encoder with a contrastive objective over subgraphs across community boundaries. Experiments on four datasets (Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD) compare GraphGuard with a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly. Table 1 reports area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP), averaged over five seeds, with standard deviations of at most 0.006. GraphGuard achieves the highest mean AUC and AP on all four datasets. Against SubgraphAnomaly on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, it raises AUC by 0.059 to 0.083 and AP by 0.072 to 0.087. On Cora-OOD, GraphGuard improves mean AUC by 0.003 and mean AP by 0.004 over SubgraphAnomaly; we did not test the significance of these gains. Against the GCN, GraphGuard raises AUC by 0.079 to 0.122 on all four datasets.
