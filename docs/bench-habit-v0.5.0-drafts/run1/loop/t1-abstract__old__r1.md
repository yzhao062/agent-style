Graph anomaly detection finds nodes or subgraphs that deviate from the rest of a graph. Existing approaches face two challenges: reliance on manual anomaly labels and degraded accuracy on graphs with dense community structure. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised detector that pretrains an encoder with a contrastive objective over subgraphs across community boundaries. Experiments on four datasets (Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, and Cora-OOD) compare GraphGuard with a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly. We report area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP), averaged over five seeds, with standard deviations of at most 0.006. Against SubgraphAnomaly, GraphGuard raises AUC by 0.083 on Reddit-B, 0.081 on Twitch-EN, and 0.059 on Elliptic, and raises AP by 0.087, 0.080, and 0.072 on the same datasets. On Cora-OOD, the gains shrink to 0.003 AUC and 0.004 AP, below the 0.006 bound on seed standard deviation. GraphGuard also exceeds the GCN on all four datasets, by 0.079 to 0.122 AUC.
