### Preliminary Results

We piloted FedVerify on a 100-node heterogeneous edge testbed to measure its resistance to model poisoning on diverse edge hardware. Twenty of the 100 clients (20%) acted as Byzantine clients, which send corrupted model updates to degrade the shared model. Client data were non-IID (not independent and identically distributed), using a Dirichlet partition with concentration alpha = 0.2 (Hsu et al. 2019). Smaller alpha values produce more heterogeneous client data.

We compared FedVerify with undefended FedAvg, which averages all client updates (McMahan et al. 2017). The robust baselines were Krum (Blanchard et al. 2017) and FL-Trust (Cao et al. 2021). FedAvg did not reach 80% accuracy within the evaluated training horizon and ended at 34.2%. FedVerify reached 80% accuracy in 45% fewer rounds and 50% less wall-clock time than FL-Trust, the faster robust baseline. Relative to Krum, it used 66% fewer rounds and 62% less wall-clock time. FedVerify's lower round count drives its shorter time to target. Against Krum, this reduction outweighs a higher average time per round (2.9 versus 2.6 minutes). FedVerify also ended with the highest final accuracy, 2.3 points above FL-Trust and 4.4 points above Krum.

Per-round communication for FedVerify falls between the robust baselines: 14% above Krum and 23% below FL-Trust. Using Table 4's per-round volumes, estimated communication to target is about 58% lower than FL-Trust's and 62% lower than Krum's. These estimates assume that the reported per-round volume is representative of every round up to the target.

Under the evaluated poisoning and non-IID conditions, FedVerify reached the accuracy target faster and achieved higher final accuracy than both robust baselines. Run-to-run variation is unquantified, because Table 4 gives one value per metric and no confidence intervals. The pilot also covers a single Byzantine fraction (20%), a single heterogeneity level (alpha = 0.2), and poisoning attacks only. Other Byzantine fractions, heterogeneity levels, and attack strategies remain untested.
