Table 6 reports mean test accuracy (%) and standard deviation over 10 runs for no augmentation, SMOTE, and TabMix on four datasets. The largest gain appears on Churn-small (2,000 rows), where TabMix reaches 81.7%, against 76.4% for no augmentation and 78.0% for SMOTE. TabMix improves mean accuracy by 5.3 percentage points over no augmentation and 3.7 over SMOTE; the standard deviations are 0.9 for TabMix, 1.1 for no augmentation, and 1.0 for SMOTE. On Adult, the three methods differ by at most 0.2 points, the same size as the standard deviations (0.2 to 0.3). Credit-default shows SMOTE (82.4%) and TabMix (82.3%) ahead of no augmentation (81.9%) by 0.5 and 0.4 points, against standard deviations of 0.4 to 0.5. Covertype favors no augmentation (92.6%): SMOTE loses 0.5 points and TabMix loses 0.2. Churn-small is the only small dataset we tested, so we cannot say whether the gain extends to other low-data settings.
