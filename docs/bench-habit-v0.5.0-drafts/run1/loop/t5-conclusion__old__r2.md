## Conclusion

We presented AdaptRL, a method that transfers grasping policies from simulation to a real robot by learning residual dynamics online. Residual dynamics are the difference between simulator-predicted and real-robot dynamics. AdaptRL learns this difference during deployment.

Across the five object categories in Table 5 (100 real-robot trials each), AdaptRL records the highest observed grasp success of the three methods in every category. Its mean over the five categories is 82.2%, compared with 63.0% for DAgger and 46.0% for domain randomization. AdaptRL exceeds DAgger by 11 percentage points on rigid objects (94% versus 83%) and by 18 to 24 percentage points on the other four categories. Its gains over domain randomization range from 23 percentage points on rigid objects to 44 percentage points on reflective objects.

Absolute success remains well below 100% on the hardest categories. All three methods decline in the same order, from rigid to transparent objects, and AdaptRL fails 21% of reflective-object trials and 32% of transparent-object trials. The reported evaluation covers grasping across five object categories. Transfer to other robot platforms and manipulation skills remains unverified. Future work should identify the cause of the remaining failures on transparent and reflective objects, and test whether residual dynamics learned online transfer to other robots and tasks.
