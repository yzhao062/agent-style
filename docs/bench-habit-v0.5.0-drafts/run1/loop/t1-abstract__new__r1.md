Graph anomaly detection finds nodes or subgraphs that deviate from the rest of a graph. Existing detectors either need manually labeled anomalies or degrade on graphs with dense community structure. In such graphs, groups of nodes (communities) link far more tightly to each other than to the rest of the graph. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised graph anomaly detector that pretrains an encoder with a contrastive objective comparing subgraphs across community boundaries. Experiments on four datasets (Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, Cora-OOD) compare GraphGuard with a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly. We report mean area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP) over five seeds (Table 1). GraphGuard outperforms the GCN on all four datasets, by 0.079 to 0.122 AUC and 0.075 to 0.121 AP. It also outperforms SubgraphAnomaly on Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, by 0.059 to 0.083 AUC and 0.072 to 0.087 AP. On Cora-OOD, the gains over SubgraphAnomaly are small (0.003 AUC, 0.004 AP), below the largest seed standard deviation of 0.006.
