Graph anomaly detection finds nodes or subgraphs that deviate from the rest of a graph. Existing detectors either need manually labeled anomalies or degrade on graphs with dense community structure. Such graphs contain communities, groups of nodes that connect more densely within the group than across community boundaries. We introduce GraphGuard, an unsupervised graph anomaly detector that pretrains an encoder with a contrastive objective comparing subgraphs across community boundaries. Experiments on four datasets (Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, Elliptic, Cora-OOD) compare GraphGuard with a graph convolutional network (GCN) and SubgraphAnomaly. We report mean area under the ROC curve (AUC) and average precision (AP) over five seeds (Table 1), with standard deviations of at most 0.006. On Reddit-B, Twitch-EN, and Elliptic, GraphGuard improves AUC and AP over both baselines, raising AUC by 0.06 to 0.08 over SubgraphAnomaly. The margin narrows on Cora-OOD. GraphGuard outperforms GCN there by 0.079 AUC but SubgraphAnomaly by only 0.003 AUC and 0.004 AP, and we make no significance claim for that small gap.
