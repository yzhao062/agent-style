### Preliminary Results

We piloted FedVerify on a 100-node heterogeneous edge testbed to measure its resistance to model poisoning on diverse edge hardware. Twenty of the 100 clients (20%) acted as Byzantine clients, which send corrupted model updates to degrade the shared model. Client data were non-IID (not independent and identically distributed). FedAvg did not reach 80% accuracy within the evaluated training horizon and ended at 34.2%. FedVerify reached 80% accuracy in 115 rounds, against 210 for FL-Trust and 340 for Krum, and halved FL-Trust's wall-clock time to target (5.6 h versus 11.2 h). It also recorded the highest final accuracy, 85.4% (Table 4).

Per-round communication for FedVerify falls between the robust baselines (14.1 MB, versus 12.4 MB for Krum and 18.2 MB for FL-Trust). Multiplying Table 4's per-round volume by rounds to target gives communication to target roughly 60% lower than both baselines, assuming the per-round volume is representative of every round up to the target.

Table 4 reports neither replicate counts nor uncertainty estimates, so the repeatability of the observed advantages cannot be assessed. The pilot also covers a single Byzantine fraction (20%), a single heterogeneity level (alpha = 0.2), and poisoning attacks only. Other Byzantine fractions, heterogeneity levels, and attack strategies remain untested.
