Table 7 shows that TrafficCast's advantage over the strongest baseline, GraphWaveNet, shrinks as the sensor network gets smaller. On the two metropolitan networks, TrafficCast cuts 60-minute MAE by 11.6% on Metro-A (3.53 to 3.12 km/h) and by 10.6% on Metro-B (3.30 to 2.95 km/h). Both margins are at least eight times the largest seed standard deviation. The gap falls to 0.02 km/h on Coastal (640 sensors), within one standard deviation, so five seeds cannot separate the two models. Rural-Net (180 sensors) reverses the ordering: TrafficCast has the highest error of the three learned models, 3.21 km/h against 3.02 for GraphWaveNet and 3.05 for DCRNN. These results suggest that TrafficCast helps most on large sensor graphs. Because network size is confounded with road type in our benchmarks, we cannot attribute the trend to sensor count alone. The accuracy gain also costs inference time: TrafficCast takes 41 ms per batch, 12 ms (41%) longer than GraphWaveNet. On this evidence, TrafficCast is the better choice for networks the size of Metro-A and Metro-B, while GraphWaveNet matches or beats it on networks the size of Coastal and Rural-Net at lower latency.
