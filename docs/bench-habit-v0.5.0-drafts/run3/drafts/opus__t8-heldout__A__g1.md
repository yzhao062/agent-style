Many global climate models output precipitation at about 100 km resolution, which is too coarse for catchment-scale flood and water-resource studies. We present ClimaDown, a diffusion model that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km resolution. Because it samples from a learned distribution, ClimaDown returns an ensemble of plausible fine-scale fields for each coarse input. We compare ClimaDown with bias-corrected spatial disaggregation (BCSD), a statistical downscaling method, and with a U-Net baseline in four regions. On the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS; lower is better), ClimaDown has the lowest mean score of the three methods in every region. Relative to BCSD, it lowers CRPS by 0.49 in the Alpine region, 0.57 in the Monsoon region, and 0.31 in the Sahel. All three 95% bootstrap intervals exclude zero. In the Mediterranean, the difference of -0.07 has an interval of [-0.19, 0.05], which includes zero. These gains come with two drawbacks. ClimaDown underestimates 99th-percentile daily precipitation by 18% on average, twice the 9% underestimate of the U-Net. It also takes 3.1 GPU-days to train, compared with 0.4 for the U-Net. Our main target for future work is reducing the bias in extreme intensity.
