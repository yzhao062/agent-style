Daily precipitation at 100 km resolution is too coarse for catchment-scale flood and water-resource planning. We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km by sampling fine-grid fields conditioned on the coarse input. In four regions (Alpine, Monsoon, Mediterranean, and Sahel), we compare it with bias-correction spatial disaggregation (BCSD), a standard statistical method, and with a U-Net. Our metric is the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), which measures how far a predicted distribution lies from the observed value (lower is better). Relative to BCSD, ClimaDown lowers CRPS by 0.49 (Alpine), 0.57 (Monsoon), and 0.31 (Sahel), and all three 95% bootstrap intervals exclude zero. In the Mediterranean region, the difference is -0.07 with an interval of [-0.19, 0.05], which includes zero. ClimaDown also has lower CRPS than the U-Net in every region, by margins of 0.02 to 0.26. These gains carry two costs. For 99th-percentile daily precipitation, ClimaDown underestimates intensity by 18% on average, twice the 9% underestimate of the U-Net. Training takes 3.1 GPU-days, against 0.4 for the U-Net. ClimaDown suits applications that prioritize the full daily distribution over extreme intensities.
