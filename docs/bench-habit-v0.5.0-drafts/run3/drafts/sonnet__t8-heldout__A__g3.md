Coarse 100 km precipitation fields hide the local variation that hydrological impact studies require. We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km resolution. In four regions (Alpine, Monsoon, Mediterranean, Sahel), we compare it with bias correction and spatial disaggregation (BCSD) and a U-Net. The metric is the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS, lower is better). ClimaDown has the lowest CRPS of the three methods in every region. Its margin over the U-Net ranges from 0.02 in the Mediterranean to 0.26 in Alpine, without an interval. Relative to BCSD, it lowers CRPS by 0.49 in Alpine (95% bootstrap interval -0.61 to -0.37), 0.57 in Monsoon (-0.72 to -0.42), and 0.31 in Sahel (-0.44 to -0.18). In the Mediterranean, the difference is -0.07 (-0.19 to 0.05); the interval includes zero, leaving the comparison unresolved. ClimaDown has two costs relative to the U-Net. For 99th-percentile daily precipitation, it underestimates intensity by 18% on average, versus 9% for the U-Net. Training takes 3.1 GPU-days, versus 0.4 for the U-Net.
