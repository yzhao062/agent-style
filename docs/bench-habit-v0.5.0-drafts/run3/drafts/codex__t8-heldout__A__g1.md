We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model for probabilistic downscaling of daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km resolution. Our evaluation compares ClimaDown with bias correction and spatial disaggregation (BCSD) and a U-Net across Alpine, Monsoon, Mediterranean, and Sahel regions. We measure probabilistic prediction quality using the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), where lower values indicate better predictions. ClimaDown achieves CRPS values of 1.92, 3.30, 1.29, and 2.64, respectively, yielding lower point estimates than both baselines in every region. Relative to BCSD, CRPS decreases by 0.49 in Alpine, 0.57 in Monsoon, and 0.31 in Sahel. The corresponding 95% bootstrap intervals exclude zero, supporting improvements in these three regions. In Mediterranean, the difference is -0.07, with a 95% bootstrap interval of [-0.19, 0.05], leaving the improvement uncertain. For 99th-percentile daily precipitation, ClimaDown underestimates intensity by 18% on average, compared with 9% for U-Net. Training requires 3.1 GPU-days, versus 0.4 for U-Net. These results show regional gains in probabilistic prediction quality alongside greater underestimation of extreme precipitation and higher training cost, motivating further work on extreme-intensity accuracy and computational efficiency.
