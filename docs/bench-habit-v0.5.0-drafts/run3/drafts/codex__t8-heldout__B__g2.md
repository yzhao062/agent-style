We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km resolution. Our evaluation compares probabilistic accuracy, extreme precipitation intensity, and training cost against statistical and neural baselines. We assess ClimaDown against bias correction and spatial disaggregation (BCSD) and a U-Net across Alpine, Monsoon, Mediterranean, and Sahel regions. Probabilistic accuracy uses the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), for which lower values indicate better predictions. ClimaDown reduces CRPS relative to BCSD by approximately 11% to 20% in Alpine, Monsoon, and Sahel, with 95% bootstrap intervals excluding zero (Table 8). The Mediterranean difference is small and statistically inconclusive, with its interval spanning zero. Across all four regions, ClimaDown achieves lower point estimates of CRPS than U-Net. For 99th-percentile daily precipitation, ClimaDown underestimates intensity by 18% on average, twice the 9% underestimation of U-Net. Training requires 3.1 GPU-days, compared with 0.4 for U-Net. These results support regional gains in probabilistic accuracy while identifying a tradeoff: ClimaDown incurs greater training cost and larger extreme-intensity bias than U-Net.
