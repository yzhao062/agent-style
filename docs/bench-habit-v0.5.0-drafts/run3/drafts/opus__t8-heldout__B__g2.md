Global climate models simulate precipitation on roughly 100 km grids, too coarse to resolve the terrain and storms that shape local rainfall. We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km and samples an ensemble of plausible high-resolution fields for each coarse input. As baselines, we use bias-corrected spatial disaggregation (BCSD), a standard statistical method, and a U-Net neural network. Our metric is the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), which measures the distance between the predicted distribution and the observation (lower is better). Against BCSD, ClimaDown lowers CRPS by 20% (Alpine), 15% (Monsoon), and 11% (Sahel), with 95% bootstrap intervals that exclude zero. In the Mediterranean, the 5% reduction is within noise because its interval includes zero. ClimaDown also has a lower CRPS than the U-Net in every region, by 0.02 (Mediterranean) to 0.26 (Alpine). The gains come with two costs. ClimaDown underestimates the intensity of 99th-percentile daily precipitation by 18% on average, twice the U-Net's 9%. Training takes 3.1 GPU-days, nearly eight times the U-Net's 0.4. For studies of extreme rainfall, the U-Net remains the more accurate choice.
