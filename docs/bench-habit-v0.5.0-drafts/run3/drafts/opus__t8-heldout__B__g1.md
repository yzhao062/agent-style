Global climate models typically simulate precipitation on grids of about 100 km, too coarse for flood and water-supply planning in individual catchments. We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km resolution by sampling an ensemble of plausible high-resolution fields for each coarse input. In four regions (Alpine, Monsoon, Mediterranean, and Sahel), we compare it with bias-corrected spatial disaggregation (BCSD), a standard statistical method, and with a deterministic U-Net. The metric is the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), which measures how far a predicted distribution lies from the observed value; lower is better. ClimaDown has the lowest mean CRPS of the three methods in every region. Relative to BCSD, it lowers CRPS by 11-20% in the Alpine, Monsoon, and Sahel regions, where 95% bootstrap intervals exclude zero. In the Mediterranean, its 5% reduction is within noise (the interval spans zero). ClimaDown is weaker on extremes: it underestimates 99th-percentile daily precipitation by 18% on average, twice the 9% underestimate of the U-Net. Training also costs more: 3.1 GPU-days versus 0.4 for the U-Net. For studies centered on extreme rainfall, the U-Net therefore remains the more accurate and cheaper choice.
