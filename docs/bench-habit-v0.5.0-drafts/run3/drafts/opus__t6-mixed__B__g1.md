Table 6 reports accuracy for TabMix, SMOTE, and no augmentation, averaged over 10 runs. TabMix shows a clear gain on one dataset: Churn-small, the smallest, with 2,000 rows. It reaches 81.7% accuracy there, which is 5.3 points above no augmentation and 3.7 points above SMOTE. Both margins are several times the run-to-run standard deviation of about 1 point. On Adult and Credit-default, SMOTE leads TabMix by 0.1 points, well within one standard deviation, so the two methods tie. Both augmentations raise Credit-default accuracy by 0.4 to 0.5 points over no augmentation, roughly one standard deviation, and change Adult accuracy by at most 0.2 points. Covertype is the one dataset where augmentation hurts: compared with no augmentation, TabMix lowers accuracy by 0.2 points and SMOTE by 0.5 points. Churn-small is the only small dataset we evaluated, so the evidence that TabMix helps most when training data are scarce rests on this single result.
