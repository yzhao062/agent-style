Table 6 reports mean accuracy over 10 runs for TabMix, SMOTE, and no augmentation on four datasets. TabMix gives its only clear gain on Churn-small, the 2,000-row dataset. There it reaches 81.7% accuracy, 3.7 points above SMOTE and 5.3 points above no augmentation. Both gaps are several times the run-to-run standard deviation of about 1 point. On the three larger datasets, TabMix changes accuracy by at most 0.4 points relative to no augmentation. All three methods on Adult fall within 0.2 points of each other, inside one standard deviation. Credit-default shows TabMix and SMOTE improving on no augmentation by roughly one standard deviation (0.4 and 0.5 points), and the two methods are within 0.1 points of each other. Covertype is the one dataset where augmentation lowers accuracy: SMOTE costs 0.5 points and TabMix 0.2 points relative to the unaugmented baseline. These results suggest that TabMix is most useful when training data is scarce, though Churn-small is the only small dataset in this evaluation.
