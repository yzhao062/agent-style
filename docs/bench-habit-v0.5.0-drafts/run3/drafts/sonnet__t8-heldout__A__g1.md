Climate impact studies need daily precipitation at 10 km resolution, yet global climate models produce it at about 100 km. ClimaDown downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km with a diffusion model, a generative network that draws samples by iteratively removing noise. We compare it with BCSD (bias correction and spatial disaggregation) and a U-Net in four regions, scoring each with the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS; lower is better). Relative to BCSD, ClimaDown lowers CRPS by 0.49 in Alpine, 0.57 in Monsoon, and 0.31 in Sahel. The 95% bootstrap intervals for these differences are -0.61 to -0.37, -0.72 to -0.42, and -0.44 to -0.18. In the Mediterranean region, the difference of -0.07 has an interval of -0.19 to 0.05 that includes zero, so the data are consistent with no improvement there. ClimaDown also reaches lower CRPS than the U-Net in all four regions; we report intervals only for the comparison with BCSD. Two limitations remain. ClimaDown underestimates 99th-percentile daily precipitation intensity by 18% on average, versus 9% for the U-Net, and it needs 3.1 GPU-days to train, versus 0.4.
