Table 7 shows that TrafficCast's advantage shrinks as networks get smaller and reverses on the smallest one. Compared with GraphWaveNet, the strongest baseline, TrafficCast lowers 60-minute MAE by 0.41 km/h (11.6%) on Metro-A (2,100 sensors) and 0.35 km/h (10.6%) on Metro-B (1,450 sensors). Both gaps are more than eight times the seed standard deviation. On Coastal (640 sensors), the 0.02 km/h difference falls within either model's standard deviation, so TrafficCast and GraphWaveNet tie. Rural-Net (180 sensors) reverses the ranking: TrafficCast's MAE of 3.21 km/h trails GraphWaveNet (3.02 km/h) by about two seed standard deviations and also trails DCRNN (3.05 km/h). We do not yet know what causes this reversal; one candidate that a targeted ablation could test is overfitting on graphs with few sensors. With only four networks, we also cannot locate the size at which TrafficCast's advantage disappears. TrafficCast is slower at inference, taking 41 ms per batch against 29 ms for GraphWaveNet, a 41% increase. For networks the size of Metro-A and Metro-B, TrafficCast is the most accurate model we tested. At the scale of Coastal or Rural-Net, GraphWaveNet matches or beats it with lower latency.
