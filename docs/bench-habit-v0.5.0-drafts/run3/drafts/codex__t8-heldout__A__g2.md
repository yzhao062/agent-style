We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km resolution. Across Alpine, Monsoon, Mediterranean, and Sahel regions, we compare ClimaDown with bias correction and spatial disaggregation (BCSD) and a U-Net baseline. Evaluation uses the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), for which lower values indicate better probabilistic predictions. ClimaDown achieves lower CRPS point estimates than both baselines in all four regions. Relative to BCSD, CRPS decreases by 0.49 in Alpine, 0.57 in Monsoon, and 0.31 in Sahel, with 95% bootstrap intervals excluding zero. In Mediterranean, the decrease is 0.07, with a 95% bootstrap interval of [-0.19, 0.05], leaving the improvement uncertain. For 99th-percentile daily precipitation, ClimaDown underestimates intensity by 18% on average, compared with 9% for U-Net. Training requires 3.1 GPU-days, compared with 0.4 GPU-days for U-Net. These results support improved probabilistic downscaling relative to BCSD in three evaluated regions. Further development should target the larger extreme-intensity bias and higher training cost accompanying ClimaDown’s CRPS gains.
