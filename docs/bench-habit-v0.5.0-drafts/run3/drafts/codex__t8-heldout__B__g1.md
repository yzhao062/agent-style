We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km resolution. Across Alpine, Monsoon, Mediterranean, and Sahel regions, we compare ClimaDown with bias correction and spatial disaggregation (BCSD) and a U-Net baseline. Probabilistic accuracy is measured using the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), where lower values indicate better performance. ClimaDown achieves lower CRPS point estimates than both baselines in all four regions (Table 8). Relative to BCSD, it reduces CRPS by approximately 11% to 20% in Alpine, Monsoon, and Sahel regions, with 95% bootstrap intervals excluding zero. In the Mediterranean region, the estimated reduction is small and statistically inconclusive because the interval includes zero. Performance on extreme precipitation is weaker: ClimaDown underestimates 99th-percentile daily intensity by 18% on average, compared with 9% for U-Net. Training requires 3.1 GPU-days, versus 0.4 for U-Net, approximately eight times the compute. These results establish regional improvements in probabilistic accuracy while identifying larger extreme-intensity bias and higher training cost than U-Net.
