Table 7 shows that TrafficCast achieves the lowest 60-minute mean absolute error (MAE) on the two largest networks. On Metro-A (2,100 sensors), it lowers MAE from 3.53 km/h for GraphWaveNet, the strongest baseline, to 3.12 km/h, an 11.6% reduction. Metro-B (1,450 sensors) shows a 10.6% reduction, from 3.30 to 2.95 km/h. Both gaps are at least seven times the largest seed standard deviation in those rows (0.05 km/h). The advantage shrinks on Coastal (640 sensors), where TrafficCast reaches 2.89 ± 0.06 km/h against 2.91 ± 0.05 km/h for GraphWaveNet, a difference within seed variation. On Rural-Net (180 sensors), TrafficCast ranks last among the three learned models, at 3.21 ± 0.10 km/h versus 3.02 ± 0.08 for GraphWaveNet and 3.05 ± 0.09 for DCRNN. With only four networks, we treat this trend with network size as an observation and have not isolated its cause. Inference takes 41 ms per batch for TrafficCast versus 29 ms for GraphWaveNet, which is 41% longer. The extra 12 ms per batch accompanies an MAE reduction of about 11% on Metro-A and Metro-B; on Rural-Net, GraphWaveNet is more accurate and faster.
