Table 6 reports accuracy (mean ± standard deviation over 10 runs) for training without augmentation, with SMOTE, and with TabMix. On Adult and Credit-default, TabMix and SMOTE differ by 0.1 points, which is less than either method's standard deviation, and SMOTE is slightly ahead on both. Compared with no augmentation, both methods gain 0.4 to 0.5 points on Credit-default, about one standard deviation. On Adult, neither gains more than 0.2 points. On Covertype, both augmentations lower accuracy: the unaugmented model reaches 92.6%, against 92.4% for TabMix and 92.1% for SMOTE. The clearest effect appears on Churn-small, which has 2,000 rows. There, TabMix reaches 81.7%, a gain of 5.3 points over no augmentation and 3.7 points over SMOTE. Both gaps exceed three times the largest standard deviation on this dataset (1.1 points). These results suggest that TabMix helps most when training data are scarce; on the other three datasets it stays within 0.3 points of SMOTE. Churn-small is the only small dataset in Table 6, however, so this pattern needs confirmation on additional low-data benchmarks.
