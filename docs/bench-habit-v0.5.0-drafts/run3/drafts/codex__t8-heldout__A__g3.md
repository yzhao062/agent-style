We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model for downscaling daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km resolution. We evaluate its probabilistic accuracy against bias correction and spatial disaggregation (BCSD) and a U-Net using the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), for which lower values indicate better predictions. Across Alpine, Monsoon, Mediterranean, and Sahel regions, ClimaDown achieves CRPS values of 1.92, 3.30, 1.29, and 2.64, respectively, improving on both baselines in each region. Relative to BCSD, the reductions are 0.49 in Alpine, 0.57 in Monsoon, and 0.31 in Sahel; the corresponding 95% bootstrap intervals exclude zero. The Mediterranean reduction is smaller at 0.07, with an interval spanning zero, leaving the improvement uncertain. Performance on precipitation extremes reveals a limitation: ClimaDown underestimates 99th-percentile daily precipitation intensity by 18% on average, compared with 9% for U-Net. Training also requires 3.1 GPU-days, compared with 0.4 for U-Net. These results show improved regional probabilistic accuracy alongside greater underestimation of extreme precipitation and higher training cost, motivating further work on extreme-intensity fidelity and computational efficiency.
