Climate model output at 100 km resolution is too coarse for catchment-scale flood and water-resource planning. We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km and generates multiple high-resolution samples for each coarse input. Our evaluation compares ClimaDown with bias-corrected spatial disaggregation (BCSD), a standard statistical method, and with a U-Net in four regions: Alpine, Monsoon, Mediterranean, and Sahel. The metric is the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), where lower is better. ClimaDown has the lowest CRPS in every region. Relative to BCSD, it reduces CRPS by 0.49 in the Alpine region, 0.57 in the Monsoon region, and 0.31 in the Sahel. All three 95% bootstrap intervals exclude zero. The Mediterranean reduction of 0.07 is inconclusive, since its interval spans [-0.19, 0.05]. Point-estimate gains over the U-Net are smaller, from 0.02 to 0.26. ClimaDown has two weaknesses. It underestimates 99th-percentile daily precipitation by 18% on average, twice the U-Net's 9%, and it needs 3.1 GPU-days of training versus 0.4 for the U-Net. These results suggest that diffusion downscaling improves probabilistic skill over BCSD in three of four regions, but flood-risk applications will require better reproduction of extremes.
