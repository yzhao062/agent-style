We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model for probabilistic downscaling of daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km resolution. Our evaluation compares distributional accuracy, extreme precipitation intensity, and training cost against bias correction and spatial disaggregation (BCSD) and a U-Net. We assess distributional accuracy using the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), for which lower values indicate better predictions. ClimaDown records lower CRPS than both baselines in all four evaluated regions (Table 8). Relative to BCSD, reductions range from 11% to 20% in the Alpine, Monsoon, and Sahel regions, with 95% bootstrap intervals for the differences excluding zero. In the Mediterranean region, the approximately 5% reduction remains statistically inconclusive because the interval includes zero. For 99th-percentile daily precipitation, ClimaDown underestimates intensity by 18% on average, compared with 9% for U-Net. Training requires 3.1 GPU-days, compared with 0.4 for U-Net, approximately eight times the compute. ClimaDown therefore improves CRPS across several regional precipitation regimes while incurring greater extreme-intensity bias and training cost than U-Net. These tradeoffs matter when choosing a downscaling model for applications that prioritize extreme precipitation or computational efficiency.
