Climate models produce daily precipitation on grids near 100 km, which limits local impact analysis. We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km resolution. Against bias correction and spatial disaggregation (BCSD) and a U-Net, we measure the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS, lower is better) in four regions. ClimaDown has the lowest CRPS in all four regions. Relative to BCSD, it reduces CRPS in Alpine (2.41 to 1.92), Monsoon (3.87 to 3.30), and Sahel (2.95 to 2.64), with 95% bootstrap intervals that exclude zero. In the Mediterranean, the reduction from 1.36 to 1.29 has an interval of [-0.19, 0.05], so the data cannot distinguish ClimaDown from BCSD there. Intense rainfall remains a weakness: for 99th-percentile daily precipitation, ClimaDown underestimates intensity by 18% on average, versus 9% for the U-Net. Training also costs more, at 3.1 GPU-days versus 0.4 GPU-days for the U-Net. Bootstrap intervals cover only the comparison with BCSD, so ClimaDown's CRPS margins over the U-Net (0.02 to 0.26) lack a significance test. Reducing the 99th-percentile underestimation and the training cost is future work.
