Global climate models produce daily precipitation at roughly 100 km resolution, too coarse to resolve local rainfall patterns. We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model (a generative model that samples by iteratively removing noise) that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km. Baselines are bias-corrected spatial disaggregation (BCSD), a statistical method, and a U-Net, a convolutional neural network. Evaluation spans four regions (Alpine, Monsoon, Mediterranean, Sahel) and uses the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS; lower is better). In the Alpine, Monsoon, and Sahel regions, ClimaDown lowers CRPS relative to BCSD by 0.31 to 0.57, with 95% bootstrap intervals that exclude zero (Table 8). The Mediterranean difference is within noise: the reduction of 0.07 has a 95% interval of -0.19 to 0.05. Mean CRPS is also lower for ClimaDown than for U-Net in all four regions (by 0.02 in the Mediterranean), but Table 8 gives no interval for that comparison. ClimaDown is weaker on extremes: at the 99th percentile of daily precipitation, it underestimates intensity by 18% on average, versus 9% for U-Net. Training ClimaDown takes 3.1 GPU-days, roughly eight times the 0.4 for U-Net.
