Climate model output at 100 km resolution is too coarse for catchment-scale flood and water-resource planning. We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model that generates ensembles of daily precipitation at 10 km resolution conditioned on 100 km fields. Across four regions, we compare it with bias-correction spatial disaggregation (BCSD), a standard statistical method, and a U-Net, scoring each with the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS). ClimaDown reduces CRPS relative to BCSD by 20% in the Alpine region, 15% in the Monsoon region, and 11% in the Sahel, with 95% bootstrap intervals excluding zero. In the Mediterranean, the 5% reduction is within noise: its 95% interval runs from -0.19 to 0.05. ClimaDown also has lower mean CRPS than the U-Net in every region, by 6-12% in the Alpine and Monsoon regions but only 2-3% elsewhere. These gains come with two costs. ClimaDown underestimates 99th-percentile daily precipitation by 18% on average, twice the U-Net's 9% bias, a limitation for extreme-event applications. Training also takes 3.1 GPU-days, nearly eight times the U-Net's 0.4.
