Climate models run at roughly 100 km resolution, too coarse to resolve local daily precipitation. We introduce ClimaDown, a diffusion model (a generative model that samples by iteratively removing noise) that downscales daily precipitation from 100 km to 10 km. Evaluation covers four regions and two baselines: bias correction and spatial disaggregation (BCSD) and a U-Net. The metric is the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), where lower values mean better probabilistic forecasts. Relative to BCSD, ClimaDown lowers CRPS by 0.49 in the Alpine region, 0.57 in the Monsoon region, and 0.31 in the Sahel region. The 95% bootstrap intervals exclude zero in all three. In the Mediterranean region, the 0.07 reduction lies within noise (interval -0.19 to 0.05). ClimaDown also has lower CRPS than the U-Net in every region, by 0.02 to 0.26, but we test only the difference from BCSD. Extremes remain a weakness: for 99th-percentile daily precipitation, ClimaDown underestimates intensity by 18% on average, twice the U-Net's 9%. Training takes 3.1 GPU-days, versus 0.4 for the U-Net.
