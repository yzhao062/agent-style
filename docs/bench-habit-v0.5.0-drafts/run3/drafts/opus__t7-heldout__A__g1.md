Table 7 shows that TrafficCast's advantage over DCRNN and GraphWaveNet shrinks as the sensor network gets smaller and reverses on the smallest one. Against GraphWaveNet, the strongest baseline, TrafficCast lowers 60-minute MAE by 0.41 km/h on Metro-A (3.53 to 3.12, 11.6%) and by 0.35 km/h on Metro-B (3.30 to 2.95, 10.6%). Each gap is more than eight times the larger of the two models' standard deviations across seeds. Coastal (640 sensors) is a tie, because the 0.02 km/h gap is smaller than either model's standard deviation. On Rural-Net (180 sensors), TrafficCast is the least accurate learned model: it reaches 3.21 km/h, against 3.05 for DCRNN and 3.02 for GraphWaveNet. TrafficCast's gains also cost latency, since it takes 41 ms per inference batch, 12 ms (41%) more than GraphWaveNet. The four networks differ in more than sensor count, so these results cannot establish network size as the cause of the trend. They do support using TrafficCast on networks of 1,450 sensors or more; on networks of a few hundred sensors, GraphWaveNet matches or beats it at lower latency.
